2 · Reality: Soul, Matter and Karma

This part is about what Jainism says is real. There are souls (jiva), which know and feel, and non-soul things (ajiva) such as matter, space and time. Karma is fine matter that sticks to the soul when it acts with attachment and aversion, and it can be stopped and worn away. If you see things this way, your happiness and suffering are no longer someone else’s doing. They come from your own soul, and that makes you responsible for them and also able to change them.

1. The first question: who am I, where did I come from?

सुयं मे आउसं ! तेणं भगवया एवमक्खायं– इहमेगेसिं णो सण्णा भवइ । तं जहा– पुरत्थिमाओ वा दिसाओ आगओ अहमंसि, दाहिणाओ वा दिसाओ आगओ अहमंसि, पच्चत्थिमाओ वा दिसाओ आगओ अहमंसि, उत्तराओ वा दिसाओ आगओ अहमंसि, उड्ढाओ वा दिसाओ आगओ अहमंसि, अहे दिसाओ वा आगओ अहमंसि, अण्णयरीओ दिसाओ वा अणुदिसाओ वा आगओ अहमंसि।

एवमेगेसिं णो णायं भवइ– अत्थि मे आया उववाइए, णत्थि मे आया उववाइए, के अहं आसी, के वा इओ चुओ इह पेच्चा भविस्सामि । ॥ १ ॥

O long-lived one, I have heard that the Blessed One (Bhagavan Mahavira) said this. Here, some beings have no awareness (sanna) of where they came from. They do not know whether they came from the east, the south, the west, the north, from above, from below, or from some other direction or in-between direction. So some do not know: “Does my soul (aya, atma) take birth again (uvavaie), or does it not? Who was I before? And when I leave this life, what will I become in the next?”

In short: The Agamas begin with you, not with God or the world. The first thing to ask is whether you are a soul that carries on from one life to the next.

Source: Acharang 1.1.1.1 (Shastra-parijna, first uddeshak) · scan (printed page 2)

2. Whoever knows the soul also accepts the world, karma and action

से जं पुण जाणेज्जा सहसम्मइयाए परवागरणेणं अण्णेसिं वा अंतिए सोच्चा, तं जहा– पुरत्थिमाओ वा दिसाओ आगओ अहमंसि जाव अण्णयरीओ दिसाओ वा अणुदिसाओ वा आगओ अहमंसि ।

एवमेगेसिं जं णायं भवइ अत्थि मे आया उववाइए, जो इमाओ दिसाओ वा अणुदिसाओ वा अणुसंचरइ, सव्वाओ दिसाओ सव्वाओ अणुदिसाओ जो आगओ अणुसंचरइ, सोऽहं । से आयावाई लोयावाई कम्मावाई किरियावाई । ॥ २ ॥

Some people do come to know it. One may know it by his own understanding (such as memory of a past life), from the explanation of the Tirthankara, or by hearing it from others: “I came from the east … or from some other direction or in-between direction.” So some know: “My soul takes birth again. The one who wanders through these directions and in-between directions, who has come from every direction and moves on through them, that one am I (so’ham).” Such a person believes in the soul (aya-vai), believes in the world (loya-vai), believes in karma (kamma-vai) and believes in action (kiriya-vai).

In short: These four go together. Once you accept a lasting soul, you also accept a real world it moves through, karma that carries it, and actions that create that karma.

Source: Acharang 1.1.1.2 · scan (printed page 4)

3. The world is living and non-living things

जीवा चेव अजीवा य, एस लोए वियाहिए ।
अजीव देसमागासे, अलोए से वियाहिए ॥ २ ॥

Living beings (jiva) and non-living things (ajiva): this is called the world (loka). The part of the non-living that is space (agasa) alone is called the non-world (aloka).

In short: Everything that exists is either soul or not soul. Past the edge of the inhabited world there is only empty space.

Source: Uttaradhyayan 36.2 (Jivajiva-vibhakti) · scan (printed page 366)

4. The six substances that make up the world

धम्मो अहम्मो आगासं, कालो पुग्गल जंतवो ।
एस लोगो त्ति पण्णत्तो, जिणेहिं वरदंसीहिं ॥ ७ ॥

धम्मो अहम्मो आगासं, दव्वं इक्किक्कमाहियं ।
अणंताणि य दव्वाणि, कालो पुग्गल जंतवो ॥ ८ ॥

गइ लक्खणो उ धम्मो, अहम्मो ठाण लक्खणो ।
भायणं सव्वदव्वाणं, णहं ओगाह लक्खणं ॥ ९ ॥

The medium of motion (dhamma), the medium of rest (adhamma), space (agasa), time (kala), matter (puggala, pudgala) and souls (jantu, jiva): the Jinas, who see everything, have taught that these make up the world.

Dhamma, adhamma and space are each said to be a single substance (davva, dravya). Time, matter and souls are infinite in number.

Dhamma’s mark is helping things move (gai). Adhamma’s mark is helping things stay still (thana). Space (naha) holds all substances, and its mark is giving them room (ogaha).

In short: Your soul is one of countless souls. It lives among matter in a shared space and time, and nothing in this list is a creator.

Source: Uttaradhyayan 28.7–9 (Mokshamarga-gati) · scan (printed page 138)

5. What marks the soul: knowing and experiencing

वत्तणा लक्खणो कालो, जीवो उवओग लक्खणो ।
णाणेणं दंसणेणं च, सुहेण य दुहेण य ॥ १० ॥

णाणं च दंसणं चेव, चरित्तं च तवो तहा ।
वीरियं उवओगो य, एयं जीवस्स लक्खणं ॥ ११ ॥

Time’s mark is change, the passing of things (vattana). The soul’s mark is awareness (uvaoga, upayoga). The soul is known through knowledge (nana), perception (damsana), happiness and suffering.

Knowledge, perception, conduct (caritta), austerity (tava), energy (viriya) and awareness: these are the marks of the soul.

In short: Only the soul knows and feels. Matter does neither. The book notes that no other substance has these qualities.

Source: Uttaradhyayan 28.10–11 · scan (printed page 139)

6. The nine realities (tattva)

जीवाजीवा य बंधो य, पुण्णं पावासवो तहा ।
संवरो णिज्जरा मोक्खो, संतेए तहिया णव ॥ १४ ॥

Soul (jiva), non-soul (ajiva), bondage (bandha), merit (punna, punya), demerit (pava, papa), inflow of karma (asava, ashrava), stopping the inflow (samvara), shedding karma (nijjara, nirjara) and liberation (mokkha, moksha): these nine are the true realities.

In short: This is the whole story in one verse. Soul meets matter, karma flows in and binds, then the inflow is stopped and old karma is shed until the soul is free. The next verse (28.15) says that believing in these nine is right faith (samyaktva).

Source: Uttaradhyayan 28.14 · scan (printed page 142)

7. The eight kinds of karma that keep the soul wandering

अट्ठ कम्माइं वोच्छामि, आणुपुव्विं जहक्कमं ।
जेहिं बद्धो अयं जीवो, संसारे परिवट्टइ ॥ १ ॥

णाणस्सावरणिज्जं, दंसणावरणं तहा ।
वेयणिज्जं तहा मोहं, आउकम्मं तहेव य ॥ २ ॥

णामकम्मं च गोयं च, अंतरायं तहेव य ।
एवमेयाइं कम्माइं, अट्ठेव उ समासओ ॥ ३ ॥

I will describe, one after another in their order, the eight karmas. Bound by them, this soul goes round and round in the cycle of birth and death (samsara).

They are: the karma that covers knowledge (nanavaraniya), the karma that covers perception (damsanavarana), the karma that brings pleasant and painful feeling (veyaniya), the karma that deludes (moha), the karma that fixes the lifespan (au-kamma), the karma that shapes the body and its features (nama), the karma that sets status by birth (goya, gotra), and the karma that blocks one’s powers (antaraya). In brief, these are the eight karmas.

In short: Karma covers and limits what the soul already has. It covers knowledge, perception, happiness and energy. It does not add anything new.

Source: Uttaradhyayan 33.1–3 (Karma-prakriti) · scan v.1 (printed page 313), scan v.2–3 (printed page 314)

8. Your own soul is your hell and your heaven

अप्पा णई वेयरणी, अप्पा मे कूडसामली ।
अप्पा कामदुहा धेणू, अप्पा मे णंदणं वणं ॥ ३६ ॥

अप्पा कत्ता विकत्ता य, दुहाण य सुहाण य ।
अप्पा मित्तममित्तं च, दुप्पट्ठिय-सुपट्ठिओ ॥ ३७ ॥

My own soul (appa) is the Vaitarani river and the Kutashalmali tree of hell. My own soul is the wish-fulfilling cow (kamadhenu) and the Nandana garden of heaven.

The soul is the maker and the unmaker of its own sufferings and joys. Set on a good path, the soul is its own friend. Set on a bad path, it is its own enemy.

In short: No god, fate or other person is finally to blame for your suffering or praised for your happiness. The monk Anathi says this to King Shrenik to explain what it really means to have no protector.

Source: Uttaradhyayan 20.36–37 (Mahanirgranthiya) · scan (printed page 425)

9. How karma is stopped and shed: the lake that dries up

जहा महातलायस्स, सण्णिरुद्धे जलागमे ।
उस्सिंचणाए तवणाए, कमेणं सोसणा भवे ॥ ५ ॥

एवं तु संजयस्सावि, पावकम्म णिरासवे ।
भवकोडी संचियं कम्मं, तवसा णिज्जरिज्जइ ॥ ६ ॥

A big lake dries up little by little once its inflowing channels are blocked, the water is drawn out and the sun heats it.

In the same way, once a restrained person (samjaya) has stopped the inflow of bad karma, the karma gathered over millions of births is shed (nijjarijjai) through austerity (tava).

In short: First close the channels (samvara), then dry out what is already there (nirjara). Austerity does little good while new karma keeps flowing in.

Source: Uttaradhyayan 30.5–6 (Tapomarga-gati) · scan (printed page 226)

10. Putting it together: the four tools that free the soul

You met Uttaradhyayan 28.35–36 in Foundations §9–10. Read it again now. Knowledge and faith show you the nine realities above, while conduct closes the inflow (samvara) and austerity burns off what is stored (nirjara).

Go deeper

  • Uttaradhyayan ch. 28 (Mokshamarga-gati), read in full. It covers the five kinds of knowledge, substance, quality and mode (dravya, guna, paryaya), the marks of matter (28.12–13), the kinds of right faith and conduct, and the twelve austerities.
  • Uttaradhyayan ch. 33 (Karma-prakriti). It lists the sub-types of each of the eight karmas and explains how long karma stays bound and how strongly it acts.
  • Uttaradhyayan ch. 36 (Jivajiva-vibhakti). This is the full map of non-soul (the kinds of matter, atoms and clusters) and of souls (liberated souls, siddha-shila, and the living beings of earth, water, fire, air and plants, mobile beings, hell-beings, animals, humans and gods).
  • Acharang Book 1, ch. 1 (Shastra-parijna). After the opening on the soul it goes through the six classes of living beings one by one, and it is the root of ahimsa.
  • Uttaradhyayan ch. 30 (Tapomarga-gati) and ch. 34 (Leshya). Chapter 30 describes the external and internal austerities that shed karma, and chapter 34 describes the soul’s “colourings” (leshya) that go with its thoughts.
