5 · Relationships: Forgiveness, Friendship, Respect

The Agamas say a great deal about how to live with others, not only how to live with yourself. Their teaching runs in one line: hold friendship toward every living being, ask and give forgiveness without holding anything back, be humble (vinaya) toward those who teach you, speak with care, and judge people by what they do rather than the family they were born into. Alongside this they say plainly that family and wealth, however dear, cannot carry your karma for you. This is the side of Jainism you use every day, at home, at work and on Samvatsari.

1. Hold friendship toward all beings

समिक्ख पंडिए तम्हा, पास जाइपहे बहू ।
अप्पणा सच्चमेसिज्जा, मेत्तिं भूएहिं कप्पए ॥२॥

Therefore the wise person, having looked at the many paths of birth (the countless forms in which beings are born and die) and thought about them, should seek the truth by his own self, and should hold friendship (maitri) toward all beings.

In short: Every creature is on the same round of birth and death as you are. Seeing that clearly leads to friendship with all of them, not just the people you like.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra 6.2 · scan (printed page 114)

2. Ask forgiveness even for harm you did without noticing

इच्छामि पडिक्कमिउं इरियावहियाए विराहणाए गमणागमणे पाणक्कमणे बीयक्कमणे हरियक्कमणे ओसा उत्तिंग पणग दग मट्टी मक्कडा संताणा संकमणे जे मे जीवा विराहिया एगिंदिया बेइंदिया तेइंदिया चउरिंदिया पंचिंदिया अभिहया वत्तिया लेसिया संघाइया संघट्टिया परियाविया किलामिया उद्दविया ठाणाओठाणं संक्कामिया जीवियाओ ववरोविया तस्स मिच्छामि दुक्कडम् ।

I wish to turn back (pratikraman) from the harm done while walking, in going and coming: in treading on living creatures, on seeds, on green plants, on dew, ant-holes, fungus, living water and living earth, and spiders’ webs. Whatever beings I have harmed, with one sense, two, three, four or five senses: if I have struck them, covered them with dust, crushed them against the ground, heaped them together, touched them painfully, distressed them, wearied them, frightened them, moved them from one place to another, or taken their life, may that wrong of mine be fruitless (micchami dukkadam).

In short: “Micchami dukkadam” is not a greeting; it is a real apology, and it is owed even to an ant you stepped on without seeing. If you apologise for that much, apologising to people is easy.

Source: Avashyak Sutra, Avashyak 4, Path 4 (Iriyavahiya / Gamanagaman Pratikraman Sutra) · scan (printed page 54)

3. Forgive everyone, and ask everyone to forgive you

खामेमि सव्वे जीवा, सव्वे जीवा वि खमंतु मे ।
मित्तीमे सव्व भूएसू, वेरं मज्झं ण केणइ ॥१॥

I forgive all living beings; may all living beings forgive me. I have friendship toward all beings; I have enmity toward none.

In short: Forgiveness goes both ways: you give it and you ask for it, at the same time and to everyone. The commentary says that only when enmity is gone from the heart can a person move toward freedom from attachment.

Source: Avashyak Sutra, Avashyak 4, Path 10 (Kshamapana Sutra) v.1 · scan (printed page 126)

4. What asking forgiveness does to you

खमावणयाए णं भंते ! जीवे किं जणयइ ?
खमावणयाए णं पल्हायणभावं जणयइ । पल्हायण भावमुवगए य सव्वपाण-भूय-जीव- सत्तेसु मित्तीभावमुप्पाएइ । मित्तीभावमुवगए यावि जीवे भावविसोहिं काऊण णिब्भए भवइ ।

“Lord, what does the soul gain by asking forgiveness (kshamapana)?” “By asking forgiveness the soul gains gladness of mind. With a glad mind it develops friendship toward all living things, beings, souls and creatures. Having reached friendship, it purifies its feelings and becomes free of fear.”

In short: The commentary explains the last step: once you have asked forgiveness and given it, nobody is your enemy, so there is no one left to fear. Saying sorry is good for the person who says it.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra 29.19 · scan (printed page 179)

5. Humility is the root of dharma

मूलाउ खंधप्पभवो दुमस्स, खंधाउ पच्छा समुवेंति साहा ।
साहप्पसाहा विरुहंति पत्ता, तओ सि पुप्फं च फलं रसो य ॥

एवं धम्मस्स विणओ, मूलं परमो से मोक्खो ।
जेण कित्तिं सुयं सिग्घं, णीसेसं चाभिगच्छइ ॥

From the root of a tree grows the trunk; from the trunk come the branches; from the branches the smaller branches, and then the leaves; after that come its flowers, its fruit and its juice. In the same way, humility (vinaya) is the root of dharma, and its highest fruit is liberation (moksha). Through vinaya a person gains good name, learning (shruta), praise, and everything else in full.

In short: Vinaya means humility, respect and readiness to be taught. Everything else in religious life grows out of it; without it nothing takes root.

Source: Dashvaikalik Sutra 9.2.1–2 · scan (printed page 399)

6. What a humble student looks like

आणाणिद्देसकरे, गुरुणमुववायकारए ।
इंगियागारसंपण्णे, से विणीए त्ति वुच्चइ ॥२॥

One who carries out the instructions and directions of his teachers (guru), stays close to them, and understands their signs and moods, is called humble (vinita).

In short: Respect is shown by listening, staying near and paying attention, not just by bowing. The same applies to parents and elders.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra 1.2 · scan (printed page 3)

7. When an elder is upset with you

आयरियं कुवियं णच्चा, पत्तिएण पसायए ।
विज्झवेज्ज पंजलिउडो, वएज्ज ण पुणो त्ति य ॥४१॥

Knowing that the teacher (acharya) is angry, the student should win back his good will with loving words, calm him with folded hands, and say: “I will not do it again.”

In short: Do not argue back or sulk. Own your part, apologise in gentle words, and promise to do better; that is forgiveness put into practice.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra 1.41 · scan (printed page 24)

8. Speak only when needed, never falsely, and take hard words well

णापुट्ठो वागरे किंचि, पुट्ठो वा णालियं वए ।
कोहं असच्चं कुव्विज्जा, धारिज्जा पियमप्पियं ॥१४॥

Do not say anything unless asked; when asked, do not tell a lie. If anger arises, make it fruitless (let it go before it acts). Take both pleasant and unpleasant words (from the teacher) as if they were pleasant.

In short: The commentary tells of a mother whose son had been killed; when the killer begged for refuge, she told her other son to let him go, because anger does not have to be acted on. Hard words from people who mean you well are a gift, not an insult.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra 1.14 · scan (printed page 10)

9. Even the truth must not be used to wound

Dashvaikalik 7.11–12 teaches that true words spoken to hurt (calling someone “one-eyed”, “diseased” or “thief”) are still wrong speech. The verses are in Conduct §8.

10. People are worth what they do, not what they are born as

समयाए समणो होइ, बंभचेरेण बंभणो ।
णाणेण य मुणी होइ, तवेण होइ तावसो ॥

कम्मुणा बंभणो होइ, कम्मुणा होइ खत्तिओ ।
वइस्सो कम्मुणा होइ, सुद्दो हवइ कम्मुणा ॥

One becomes a shramana by equanimity (samata), a brahmin by celibacy (brahmacharya), a muni by knowledge, and an ascetic (tapasa) by austerity (tapa). One becomes a brahmin by one’s actions, a kshatriya by one’s actions, a vaishya by one’s actions, and a shudra by one’s actions.

In short: The verse just before says a shaven head, chanting “Om”, living in a forest or wearing bark does not make anyone holy. Judge people, including yourself, by conduct, never by caste, family or outward signs. The commentary adds that all humans are equal and form one kind.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra 25.32–33 · scan 25.32 (printed page 91) · scan 25.33 (printed page 92)

11. Love your family, but know they cannot carry your karma

माया पिया ण्हुसा भाया, भज्जा पुत्ता य ओरसा ।
णालं ते तव ताणाय, लुप्पंतस्स सकम्मुणा ॥३॥

ण तस्स दुक्खं विभयंति णाइओ, ण मित्तवग्गा ण सुया ण बंधवा।
इक्को सयं पच्चणुहोइ दुक्खं, कत्तारमेव अणुजाइ कम्मं ॥२३॥

Mother, father, daughter-in-law, brother, wife, and your own sons: none of them can protect you when you suffer from your own karma. Kinsmen cannot share his suffering, nor can friends, sons or relatives. He alone experiences his suffering himself; karma follows only the one who did it.

In short: This is not a call to love family less. It warns against doing wrong “for the family” or for wealth, because the results of that wrong will be yours alone.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra 6.3 · scan (printed page 114); Uttaradhyayan Sutra 13.23 · scan (printed page 251)
