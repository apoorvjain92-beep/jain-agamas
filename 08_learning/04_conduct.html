4 · Conduct: Vows, Speech and Inner Discipline

Right conduct (samyak charitra) is the third jewel: knowing and believing are not enough until they change how you act, speak and react. The Agamas give a householder (shravak) a practical, graded path of twelve vows, the same path the monk walks in full through the five great vows. Underneath every vow sits the real work: careful speech and the conquest of anger, pride, deceit and greed, which the texts call conquering yourself.

1. A householder can take vows without leaving home

१५ … णो खलु अहं तहा संचाएमि मुंडे भवित्ता अगाराओ अणगारियं पव्वइत्तए । अहं णं देवाणुप्पियाणं अंतिए पंचाणुव्वइयं सत्त-सिक्खावइयं दुवालसविहं गिहिधम्मं पडिवज्जिस्सामि । अहासुहं देवाणुप्पिया! मा पडिबंधं करेह।

(Ananda, a rich householder of Vanijyagram, says to Mahavira:) ”…I am not able, as those kings and others are, to shave my head and go forth from home into homelessness. In your presence, beloved of the gods, I will take up the twelve-fold householder’s dharma: five small vows (anuvrata) and seven training vows (shikshavrata).” Mahavira replies: “Do as brings you happiness, beloved of the gods. Do not delay.”

In short: Mahavira did not demand that everyone become a monk. A layperson who keeps limited vows is a real follower of the path.

Source: Upasakdashang 1, sutra 15 · scan (printed page 15)

2. The first three small vows: no gross violence, lying or stealing

१६ तए णं से आणंदे गाहावई समणस्स भगवओ महावीरस्स अंतिए तप्पढमयाए थूलगं पाणाइवायं पच्चक्खाइ, जावज्जीवाए दुविहं तिविहेणं, ण करेमि, ण कारवेमि, मणसा वयसा कायसा ।

१७ तयाणंतरं च णं थूलगं मुसावायं पच्चक्खाइ, जावज्जीवाए दुविहं तिविहेणं ण करेमि ण कारवेमि, मणसा वयसा कायसा।

१८ तयाणंतरं च णं थूलगं अदिण्णादाणं पच्चक्खाइ, जावज्जीवाए दुविहं तिविहेणं, ण करेमि, ण कारवेमि, मणसा वयसा कायसा ।

  1. Then Ananda the householder, in the presence of Shramana Bhagavan Mahavira, first renounced gross harming of living beings (sthula pranatipata): for life, in two ways and three modes — “I will not do it and I will not have it done, by mind, by speech, by body.”
  2. After that he renounced gross false speech (sthula mrishavada), for life, in the same two ways and three modes.
  3. After that he renounced gross taking of what is not given (sthula adattadana), for life, in the same two ways and three modes.

In short: “Gross” (thulaga) means the deliberate, avoidable harm, lie or theft; the unavoidable harm of running a home and business is not yet covered. The householder gives up doing and causing; approving is left open, which the monk later closes.

Source: Upasakdashang 1, sutras 16-18 · scan (printed page 16)

3. The fourth and fifth small vows: limits on sex and on wealth

१९ तयाणंतरं च णं सदारसंतोसिए परिमाणं करेइ, णण्णत्थ एक्काए सिवाणंदाए भारियाए, अवसेसं सव्वं मेहुणविहिं पच्चक्खामि ।

२० तयाणंतरं च णं इच्छा परिमाणं करेमाणे हिरण्ण-सुवण्णविहि-परिमाणं करेइ, णण्णत्थ चउहिं हिरण्णकोडीहिं णिहाणपउत्ताहिं, चउहिं वुड्ढिपउत्ताहिं, चउहिं पवित्थर-पउत्ताहिं, अवसेसं सव्वं हिरण्णसुवण्णविहिं पच्चक्खामि ।

  1. After that he set a limit of contentment with his own wife (svadara-santosha): “Apart from my one wife Sivananda, I renounce all other sexual activity.”
  2. After that, limiting his desires (iccha-parimana), he set a limit on gold and silver: “Apart from four crore of gold coins kept as treasure, four crore put out in business, and four crore in household goods, I renounce all other gold and silver.”

In short: The fifth vow is not poverty but a ceiling you choose and then do not cross. Ananda goes on (sutras 21-24) to cap his cattle at four herds of ten thousand, his land at 500 ploughs, and his carts and vehicles at fixed numbers.

Source: Upasakdashang 1, sutras 19-20 · scan (printed page 17) · scan (printed page 19)

4. The supporting vows: limit what you consume, cut harm with no purpose

२५ तयाणंतरं च णं उवभोग-परिभोगविहिं पच्चक्खाएमाणे, उल्लणियाविहि परिमाणं करेइ । णण्णत्थ एगाए गंधकासाईए, अवसेसं सव्वं उल्लणियाविहिं पच्चक्खामि ।

४६ तयाणंतरं च णं चउव्विहं अणट्ठादंडं पच्चक्खाइ, तं जहा- अवज्झाणायरियं पमायायरियं, हिंसप्पयाणं, पावकम्मोवएसे ।

  1. After that, renouncing (beyond a limit) things of single and repeated use (upabhoga-paribhoga), he set a limit on towels: “Apart from one fragrant, reddish towel, I renounce all other towels.”
  2. After that he renounced the four kinds of purposeless harm (anartha-danda): brooding on ill (apadhyana), careless conduct (pramada), handing out the means of violence (himsa-pradana), and advising others to do harmful deeds (papa-karmopadesha).

In short: Sutra 25 opens a list of 26 daily items (tooth-sticks, fruit, oil, food, clothes, ornaments…) each given a limit. Anartha-danda is harm you get nothing from: idle worry and spite, waste, lending weapons, egging others on.

Source: Upasakdashang 1, sutras 25, 46 · scan (printed page 21) · scan (printed page 26)

5. Samayika: forty-eight minutes of equanimity, and how it goes wrong

५६ तयाणंतरं च णं सामाइयस्स समणोवासएणं पंच अइयारा [पेयाला] जाणियव्वा, ण समायरियव्वा, तं जहा- मणदुप्पणिहाणे, वयदुप्पणिहाणे, कायदुप्पणिहाणे, सामाइयस्स सइअकरणया, सामाइयस्स अणवट्ठियस्स करणया ।

After that, the lay follower (shramanopasaka) should know, and not commit, the five main faults (atichara) of the samayika vow: misdirecting the mind, misdirecting speech, misdirecting the body, forgetting to do samayika (or forgetting one is in it), and doing samayika irregularly or carelessly.

In short: Samayika is a set period of sitting in equanimity, giving up all harmful activity. The faults show what it really asks: not just sitting still, but keeping mind and tongue still too.

Source: Upasakdashang 1, sutra 56 · scan (printed page 39)

The twelve vows of a householder, in short (Ananda’s text counts them as 5 small vows + 7 training vows; later tradition splits the 7 into 3 gunavratas and 4 shikshavratas):

  1. Ahimsa anuvrata - no deliberate harm to moving beings.
  2. Satya anuvrata - no gross lies.
  3. Achaurya anuvrata - no gross theft.
  4. Svadara-santosha - sexual relations only with one’s own spouse.
  5. Iccha-parimana - a fixed limit on wealth and possessions.
  6. Dig-vrata - a limit on the distance one travels or does business in each direction.
  7. Upabhoga-paribhoga-parimana - a limit on daily consumables and durables (Ananda’s 26 items).
  8. Anartha-danda-viramana - giving up the four kinds of purposeless harm.
  9. Samayika - regular periods of equanimity.
  10. Deshavakashika - a further, short-term narrowing of area and activity (for a day or night).
  11. Paushadhopavasa - a full day of fasting and living like a monk.
  12. Atithi-samvibhaga - sharing food and necessities with monks and nuns.

6. The monk’s first great vow: the same vow, with no exceptions

७ पढमे भंते ! महव्वए पाणाइवायाओ वेरमणं; सव्वं भंते ! पाणाइवायं पच्चक्खामि। से सुहुमं वा बायरं वा तसं वा थावरं वा, णेव सयं पाणे अइवाएज्जा, णेवण्णेहिं पाणे अइवायावेज्जा, पाणे अइवायंते वि अण्णे ण समणुजाणेज्जा जावज्जीवाए, तिविहं तिविहेणं मणेणं वायाए काएणं ण करेमि ण कारवेमि करंतं पि अण्णं ण समणुजाणामि, तस्स भंते । पडिक्कमामि णिंदामि गरिहामि अप्पाणं वोसिरामि । पढमे भंते ! महव्वए उवट्ठिओमि सव्वाओ पाणाइवायाओ वेरमणं ।

“The first great vow (mahavrata), Bhante, is abstaining from harming living beings. Bhante, I renounce all harming of life. Whether a being is subtle or gross, moving or stationary, I will not harm it myself, I will not have others harm it, and I will not approve of others who harm it — for life, in three ways and three modes: by mind, speech and body I will not do it, not cause it, and not approve another doing it. Of that, Bhante, I step back (pratikramana), I censure it, I condemn it, and I give up that self. Bhante, I stand in the first great vow: abstaining from all harming of life.”

In short: Compare with section 2: the householder renounces gross harm in two ways; the monk renounces all harm, including to the smallest and to plants and earth-beings, in three ways. The same formula then repeats for the second to fifth great vows (all lying, all taking what is not given, all sexual activity, all possessiveness), and a sixth, not eating at night. The small vows are the great vows scaled to a life in the world.

Source: Dashvaikalik 4, sutra 7 · scan (printed page 77)

7. What makes speech right

३ असच्चमोसं सच्चं च, अणवज्जमकक्कसं ।
समुप्पेहमसंदिद्धं, गिरं भासिज्ज पण्णवं ॥

A wise person should speak what is true, or what is neither true nor false (plain practical speech), and that speech should be free of harm (anavadya), not harsh, thought over beforehand, and free of doubt or ambiguity.

In short: Four tests before speaking: does it harm, is it harsh, have I thought it through, is it clear?

Source: Dashvaikalik 7.3 · scan (printed page 292)

8. True but hurtful words are also wrong

११ तहेव फरुसा भासा, गुरुभूओवघाइणी ।
सच्चा वि सा ण वत्तव्वा, जओ पावस्स आगमो ॥

१२ तहेव काणं काणे त्ति, पंडगं पंडगे त्ति वा ।
वाहियं वावि रोगि त्ति, तेणं चोरे त्ति णो वए ॥

  1. Likewise, harsh speech that deeply wounds living beings should not be spoken even if it is true, because it brings in sin (papa).
  2. Likewise, do not call a one-eyed man “one-eyed”, a eunuch “eunuch”, a sick man “diseased”, or a thief “thief”.

In short: Truth is necessary but not sufficient. Labels that wound, even accurate ones, are a form of violence.

Source: Dashvaikalik 7.11-12 · scan (printed page 298)

9. The four passions (kashaya) and what they destroy

३७ कोहं माणं च मायं च, लोभं च पाववड्ढणं ।
वमे चत्तारि दोसे उ, इच्छंतो हियमप्पणो ॥

३८ कोहो पीइं पणासेइ, माणो विणय णासणो ।
माया मित्ताणि णासेइ, लोहो सव्व विणासणो ॥

  1. Anger (krodha), pride (mana), deceit (maya) and greed (lobha) increase sin. One who wants his own good should vomit out these four faults.
  2. Anger destroys affection, pride destroys humility (vinaya), deceit destroys friendships, greed destroys everything.

In short: Each passion has a specific casualty in daily life. Notice which relationship or quality is being eaten away and you know which passion is at work.

Source: Dashvaikalik 8.37-38 · scan (printed page 354)

10. The antidote to each passion

३९ उवसमेण हणे कोहं, माणं मद्दवया जिणे ।
मायं च अज्जवभावेण, लोभं संतोसओ जिणे ॥

४० कोहो य माणो य अणिग्गहीया, माया य लोभो य पवड्ढमाणा ।
चत्तारि एए कसिणा कसाया, सिंचंति मूलाइं पुणब्भवस्स ॥

  1. Destroy anger by calm (upashama), conquer pride by gentleness (mardava), deceit by straightforwardness (arjava), and greed by contentment (santosha).
  2. Anger and pride left unchecked, deceit and greed left growing — these four black passions water the roots of rebirth.

In short: The vows control behaviour; this verse works on the source. Practise the opposite quality, not just suppression.

Source: Dashvaikalik 8.39-40 · scan (printed page 355)

11. Master yourself first

१५ अप्पा चेव दमेयव्वो, अप्पा हु खलु दुद्दमो ।
अप्पा दंतो सुही होइ, अस्सिं लोए परत्थ य ॥१५॥

It is the self (mind and senses) that must be tamed, for the self is truly hard to tame. One whose self is tamed is happy in this world and in the next.

Source: Uttaradhyayan 1.15 · scan (printed page 11)

12. The greatest victory

३४ जो सहस्सं सहस्साणं, संगामे दुज्जए जिणे ।
एगं जिणेज्ज अप्पाणं, एस से परमो जओ ॥३४॥

३५ अप्पाणमेव जुज्झाहि, किं ते जुज्झेण बज्झओ ।
अप्पाणमेवमप्पाणं, जिणित्ता सुहमेहए ॥३५॥

३६ पंचिंदियाणि कोहं, माणं मायं तहेव लोहं च ।
दुज्जयं चेव अप्पाणं, सव्वं अप्पे जिए जियं ॥३६॥

(Nami, a king who has renounced his kingdom, answers Indra, who urges him to first conquer the kings who have not bowed to him:)
34. One may conquer a thousand thousand (ten lakh) warriors in a hard battle; but if he conquers one, his own self, that is his supreme victory.
35. Fight with yourself; what use is fighting outside? By conquering the self through the self one gains happiness.
36. The five senses, anger, pride, deceit and greed — and the self, so hard to conquer: when the self is conquered, all of these are conquered.

In short: This is where the whole chapter points. The vows, the careful speech and the four antidotes are all ways of fighting this one battle.

Source: Uttaradhyayan 9.34-36 · scan (printed page 168) · scan (printed page 169)
