Uttaradhyayan 31 · Charan Vidhi: Rules of Conduct

In one line: conduct comes down to one rule: turn away from lack of restraint and turn toward restraint. The chapter then counts up from one to thirty-three, naming what a monk must give up, take up or simply know. Each verse ends with the same promise: “that monk does not stay in the round of rebirth.”

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra Part 2, chapter 31 (21 verses), translated pages 307–328 (data/translations/uttaradhyayan-2/). 15 minutes.

How the chapter works. After an opening verse, each verse names one or more numbered groups (“the twos”, “the threes” … “the thirty-threes”). Each ends with the refrain je bhikkhū jayaī ṇiccaṃ, se ṇa acchaī maṃḍale: “the monk who always takes care over these does not stay in the round.” The commentary sorts every group into what to abandon, what to adopt and what to know. The book closes with a table of all 33 (pages 326–328).

What the chapter teaches

  1. This conduct brings happiness and has carried many across. Mahavira’s way of conduct (charan vidhi) is happy for the soul, and by living it many souls have crossed the ocean of rebirth (v.1). The commentary adds that knowledge and faith show the way, but conduct stops new karma and austerity sheds the old.
  2. One: leave lack of restraint, enter restraint (v.2). The commentary explains this as giving up the 17 kinds of lack of restraint and keeping the 17 kinds of restraint.
  3. Two: attachment and aversion. These two sins set all sinful action going; the commentary calls them seeds from which a whole banyan tree of sin grows. The monk who always holds them back does not wander (v.3).
  4. Three and four: things to drop and things to bear. Drop the three ways of harming oneself (mind, speech, body misused), the three prides (in powers, tasty food, comfort) and the three thorns (deceit, wanting rewards, wrong belief). Bear calmly the troubles that come from gods, animals and humans (v.4–5). Avoid the four kinds of idle talk (about women, food, countries, kings), the four passions, the four instincts (food, fear, sex, possession) and the two bad kinds of meditation, distressed and cruel (v.6).
  5. Five to seven: the working tools of a monk’s life. Take care over the five great vows, the five senses, the five careful practices (samiti) and the five kinds of karma-binding activity (v.7). Take care over the six soul-colours (leshya), keeping to the three bright ones. Protect the six classes of living beings, and keep the six reasons for eating and the six for not eating (v.8). Keep the seven vows about accepting alms and the seven about lodging, and stay clear of the seven fears (v.9).
  6. Eight to twelve: pride, celibacy, the ten virtues and the vowed stages. Drop the eight kinds of pride (birth, family, strength, beauty, austerity, learning, gain, wealth). Keep the nine fences of celibacy and the ten virtues of a monk: forgiveness, freedom from greed, straightforwardness, gentleness, lightness, truth, restraint, austerity, giving and celibacy (v.10). Know and teach the eleven vowed stages (pratima) of a lay follower and the twelve of a monk (v.11).
  7. Thirteen to twenty: what to avoid and what to study. Avoid the thirteen kinds of activity that bind karma, except careful walking. Protect the fourteen groups of living beings. Never become like the fifteen cruel gods who torment hell-beings, and do not hate them either (v.12). Follow the sixteen chapters of the Sutrakritanga’s first part and the seventeen kinds of restraint (v.13). Keep the eighteen-fold celibacy, learn from the nineteen chapters of the Jnata Sutra, and avoid the twenty things that disturb a monk’s calm, such as rushing, repeated anger, stirring up old quarrels and studying at the wrong time (v.14).
  8. Twenty-one to twenty-eight: serious faults, hardships and the texts on conduct. Avoid the twenty-one serious faults (shabala), such as eating at night or knowingly lying. Bear the twenty-two hardships taught in chapter 2 (v.15). Take care over the twenty-three chapters of the Sutrakritanga and the twenty-four kinds of gods, toward whom one stays neutral, wanting nothing from them (v.16). Use the twenty-five supporting practices of the great vows and the twenty-six sections of the three books on conduct (v.17). Grow the twenty-seven qualities of a monk, and follow the twenty-eight chapters of the Acharanga and Nishitha (v.18).
  9. Twenty-nine to thirty-three: the darkest wrongs and the highest goal. Avoid the twenty-nine kinds of “sinful learning” (omens, palmistry, spells and the like) and the thirty acts that bind the heaviest deluding karma. These include killing living beings by drowning or smoke, falsely claiming celibacy or learning, and slandering one’s teachers (v.19). Aim at the thirty-one qualities of the perfected ones and the thirty-two good practices (yoga-sangraha), and avoid the thirty-three kinds of disrespect toward the guru, such as walking ahead of him or interrupting his teaching (v.20).
  10. The wise monk who keeps all this is quickly freed from the whole round of rebirth (v.21).

Key verses

1 · The rule that carries across

चरणविहिं पवक्खामि, जीवस्स उ सुहावहं ।
जं चरित्ता बहू जीवा, तिण्णा संसार-सागरं ॥

I shall tell the rule of conduct, which brings happiness to the soul. By living it, many souls have crossed the ocean of rebirth.

2 · One thing to leave, one to take up

एगओ विरइं कुज्जा, एगओ य पवत्तणं ।
असंजमे णियत्तिं च, संजमे य पवत्तणं ॥

Turn away from one thing and toward another: turn away from lack of restraint, and toward restraint.

3 · The two sins

रागदोसे य दो पावे, पावकम्म-पवत्तणे ।
जे भिक्खू रुंभइ णिच्चं, से ण अच्छइ मंडले ॥

Attachment and aversion are the two sins that set sinful action going. The monk who always holds them back does not stay in the round of rebirth.

20 · The highest numbers

सिद्धाइगुण जोगेसु, तेत्तीसासायणासु य ।
जे भिक्खू जयइ णिच्चं, से ण अच्छइ मंडले ॥

The monk who always takes care over the thirty-one qualities of the perfected ones, the thirty-two good practices and the thirty-three kinds of disrespect does not stay in the round of rebirth.

21 · Quickly freed

इइ एएसु ठाणेसु, जे भिक्खू जयइ सया ।
खिप्पं सो सव्व संसारा, विप्पमुच्चइ पंडिओ ॥ त्तिबेमि ॥

The wise monk who always takes care over these points is quickly freed from the whole of worldly existence. Thus I say.
