Acharang 2.7 · Avagraha-pratima (અવગ્રહ પડિમા): Permission to Stay

In one line: a monk or nun, vowed never to take what is not given, uses no place and no thing, not even a fellow monk’s, without the owner’s permission, sets clear limits of space and time when asking, shares what he has with fellow monks, and behaves with courtesy toward everyone he lodges among.

Source: Acharang Sutra, Book 2 (Second Shrutaskandha), chapter 7 (30 sutras, 2 sections), translated pages 298–316 (data/translations/acharang-2/). The book numbers the sutras afresh in each section, so references below read chapter.section.sutra (7.2.15 = section 2, sutra 15). 10 minutes.

What the chapter teaches

  1. Avagraha means taking with permission. The introduction explains that the word has many meanings; here it chiefly means taking a thing or a place with its owner’s leave, and the limited space so granted. A monk has given up taking what is not given in thought, word and deed, so asking leave runs through his whole life. The chapter is named for the kinds of permission and the seven vows (pratima) about it (introduction, p.298).
  2. The vow behind it all. At initiation the monk resolves to be a homeless, possessionless shramana who eats what others give and does no sinful deed, and renounces all taking of what is not given. He does not take, make others take, or approve of taking anything not given, and he does not even pick up a fellow monk’s umbrella, bowl or staff without asking and without inspecting and wiping it (7.1.1).
  3. Ask with clear limits. In a rest-house, garden house, householder’s home or ascetics’ hermitage, he asks the owner or the one in charge: “We will stay here as long and in as much space as you allow; fellow monks who come will stay within the same limit, and then we will move on.” The commentary: settle exactly which floors and rooms, the ground for waste, and the time (7.1.2).
  4. Share with fellow monks. When monks of his own circle (sambhogika) arrive, he invites them to the food he himself has begged, rather than fetching fresh food for them; to worthy monks of another group he offers the stools and bedding he has begged. The commentary recalls Keshi offering Gautama a seat of straw (7.1.3–4).
  5. Return borrowed things yourself. A needle, knife, ear-pick or nail-cutter borrowed for himself he does not pass to others; he takes it back himself and returns it laid on his open palm or on the ground, not placed hand to hand (7.1.5).
  6. Places he does not ask for. Not on living or damp earth, not on wobbly posts, walls or upper floors; not a place where householders live with fire, water, women, children and animals, whose path runs through a family’s home, where the family quarrel, oil and bathe each other or go naked, or that is covered with pictures. All these disturb study and restraint (7.1.6–13).
  7. Courtesy toward ascetics of other faiths. Lodging where other ascetics or Brahmins stay, he does not move their things in or out, does not wake them, and does nothing that displeases them. The commentary adds: keep the place clean, make no noise, and do not quarrel (7.2.1–2).
  8. In a mango, sugarcane or garlic grove. With the owner’s leave he may stay there. He may accept fruit, cane or garlic only if it has no eggs or webs and has been cut up and made lifeless; whole, uncut pieces he refuses. The commentary says garlic is taken only as medicine (7.2.3–13).
  9. Seven vows for asking lodging. (1) Ask in the usual way. (2) Ask for others and stay where others asked. (3) Ask for others but not stay where others asked. (4) Not ask for others but stay where others asked. (5) Ask only for oneself. (6) Use only the grass or straw already there, else sit or squat all night. (7) Use only a stone or wooden slab already laid, else sit or squat. Whoever keeps one vow does not look down on others (7.2.14).
  10. Five owners whose leave is needed. The elders have taught five kinds of permission: of the lord of the gods (Devendra), of the king, of the householder, of the host whose house one stays in, and of fellow monks (7.2.15–16).

Key passages

7.1.1 · I renounce taking what is not given

समणे भविस्सामि अणगारे अकिंचणे अपुत्ते अपसू परदत्तभोई पावं कम्मं णो करिस्सामि त्ति समुट्ठाए; सव्वं भंते ! अदिण्णादाणं पच्चक्खामि ।

से अणुपविसित्ता गामं वा जाव रायहाणिं वा णेव सयं अदिण्णं, गिण्हेज्जा, णेवण्णेणं अदिण्णं गिण्हावेज्जा, णेवण्णं अदिण्णं गिण्हंतं पि समणुजाणेज्जा।

Having risen up with the resolve, “I will become a shramana: homeless, owning nothing, without son, without cattle, eating what others give; I will do no sinful deed,” he says: “Venerable sir, I renounce all taking of what is not given.” Entering a village or a capital, he should not himself take what is not given, nor make another take it, nor approve of another taking it.

7.1.2 · How to ask for a place

से आगंतारेसु वा, आरामगारेसु वा, गाहावइकुलेसु वा, परियावसहेसु वा अणुवीइ ओग्गहं जाएज्जा, जे तत्थ ईसरे, जे तत्थ समहिट्ठाए ते उग्गहं अणुण्णवेज्जा- कामं खलु आउसो ! अहालंदं अहापरिण्णायं वसामो, जाव आउसो; जाव आउसंतस्स ओग्गहे जाव साहम्मिया एतावताव ओग्गहं ओगिण्हिस्सामो, तेण परं विहरिस्सामो ।

In a rest-house, a garden house, a householder’s home or an ascetics’ hermitage, he should ask for a place after thinking it over, seeking leave from whoever owns it or is in charge: “Long-lived one, we will stay for as long and in as much space as you allow. For as long as your permission runs, fellow monks who come will take only that much space; beyond that we will move on.”

7.2.2 · Do not disturb other ascetics

से किं पुण तत्थ ओग्गहंसि एवोग्गहियंसि ? जे तत्थ समणाण वा माहणाण छत्तए वा जाव चम्मछेयणए वा तं णो अंतोहिंतो बाहिं णीणेज्जा, बहियाओ वा णो अंतो पवेसेज्जा, णो सुत्तं वा णं पडिबोहेज्जा, णो तेसिं किंचि अप्पत्तियं पडिणीयं करेज्जा ।

Having got leave for the place, how should he live there? If other shramanas or Brahmins keep their umbrella or other things, up to a leather-knife, there, he should not carry them from inside to outside or from outside to inside, should not wake one who sleeps, and should do nothing at all to displease or oppose them.

7.2.14 · The sixth vow: only what is already there

अहावरा छट्ठा पडिमा- से भिक्खू वा भिक्खुणी वा जस्सेव ओग्गहे उवल्लिएज्जा, जे तत्थ अहासमण्णागए तं जहा- इक्कडे वा जाव पलाले वा, तस्स लाभे संवसेज्जा, तस्स अलाभे उक्कुडुए वा णेसज्जिए वा विहरेज्जा । छट्ठा पडिमा ।

The sixth vow: in whatever place the monk or nun stays with leave, if there is grass or straw already there, he or she may use it; if there is none, he or she passes the time squatting or sitting. This is the sixth vow.

7.2.15 · The five kinds of permission

सुयं मे आउसं तेणं भगवया एवमक्खायं- इह खलु थेरेहिं भगवंतेहिं पंचविहे ओग्गहे पण्णत्ते, तं जहा- देविंदोग्गहे, रायोग्गहे, गाहावइओग्गहे, सागारियओग्गहे, साहम्मियओग्गहे ।

Long-lived one, I have heard the Lord say this: the venerable elders have taught five kinds of permission: that of the lord of the gods, of the king, of the householder, of the host, and of fellow monks.

Note: the translated page numbers this sutra 14; the print numbers it 15 (the seven vows are sutra 14), so 15 is used here.
