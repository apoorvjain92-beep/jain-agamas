Avashyak 2 · Chaturvimshatistava (બીજો આવશ્યક : ચતુર્વિંશતિસ્તવ): Praise of the 24

In one line: the seeker keeps the 24 Tirthankaras, who reached perfect equanimity, before his eyes by praising their names, and asks of them only health of the soul, right understanding and the highest calm.

Source: Avashyak Sutra, 2nd Avashyak (1 sutra: the Logassa, 7 verses), translated pages 86–95 (data/translations/avashyak/). References read v.(verse) of the Logassa, with page numbers for the preface and commentary. 10 minutes.

What the chapter teaches

  1. Praise follows equanimity because the seeker needs a model. Old habits keep pulling him back into sinful activity. As a painter first fixes the picture in his mind, the seeker fixes on the Tirthankaras, who are wholly free of it, and draws strength from remembering their names and virtues. “A person becomes what he has faith in” (p. 86).
  2. Its fruit is purer faith. In the Uttaradhyayan Sutra, Gautama asks what the soul gains from this praise; Mahavira answers that it purifies faith (darshan vishuddhi). Love of their virtues brings those virtues, and in the end the soul becomes like them (p. 86).
  3. How it is recited. Seated in yoga-mudra (hands joined, elbows on the stomach) or standing in jina-mudra (arms hanging straight, as in the Tirthankaras’ meditation). The first verse is in Anushtup metre, the other six in Arya (p. 86).
  4. A name carries its meaning. As the word “lotus” calls up the flower, Mahavira’s name calls up his patience and equanimity. The spoken word can awaken inner knowledge (p. 87).
  5. The first verse names four marks of a Tirthankara. “Illuminator of the world” (light of knowledge that never sets), “founder of the ford of dharma” (the fourfold order of monks, nuns, laymen and laywomen), “Jina” (conqueror of attachment and aversion) and “Arihant” (worthy of worship). “Kevali” adds that ordinary omniscients, who found no order, are equal to them in knowledge and conduct (v.1; pp. 90–91).
  6. The 24 names have reasons. Verses 2–4 name the 24 of this age in Bharat. The commentary, following the Niryukti, explains each name from what happened while the mother carried the child: Ajit because she always won at the board game (chopat), Shantinath because plague in the city ceased, Vardhaman because the clan’s wealth grew (v.2–4; pp. 91–93).
  7. They are free of the dust and dirt of karma, and of old age and death. Karma being bound now is “dust”, karma bound before is “dirt”, or loosely and tightly bound karma. Rooting out attachment and aversion, they bind no karma that brings another birth (v.5; pp. 93–94).
  8. “Be pleased with me”, though they are beyond pleasure. A detached being is pleased or displeased with no one. The devotee says it anyway, giving up his ego; it is the purity of his own feeling that sheds karma (v.5; pp. 93–95).
  9. He asks only for spiritual gifts. Praised in name (kirtan), bowed to (vandan) and honoured (puja), they are asked for health of the soul (freedom from the disease of birth and death), bodhi (right understanding, the seed of the three jewels) and the highest samadhi. He asks for nothing material (v.6; pp. 94–95).
  10. Purer than moons, brighter than suns, deeper than the ocean. The moon has a spot, the sun sets, the sea has waves; the Siddhas have none of these. “Grant me perfection” means: with your support may I reach it (v.7; p. 95).

Key passages

v.1 · I will praise the 24

लोगस्स उज्जोयगरे, धम्मतित्थयरे जिणे ।
अरिहंते कित्तइस्सं, चउवीसं पि केवली ॥१॥

I will praise the Arihants who light up the whole world, who found the ford of dharma, the conquerors, the omniscient ones, all twenty-four.

v.5 · Free of karma’s dust, old age and death

एवं मए अभिथुआ, विहुयरयमला पहीणजरमरणा ।
चउवीसं पि जिणवरा, तित्थयरा मे पसीयंतु ॥५॥

Thus praised by me, free of the dust and dirt of karma, rid of old age and death, may the twenty-four supreme Jinas, the Tirthankaras, be pleased with me.

v.6 · The only gifts asked

कित्तिय-वंदिय-महिया, जे ए लोगस्स उत्तमा सिद्धा ।
आरुग्ग-बोहि लाभं, समाहि-वरमुत्तमं दिंतु ॥६॥

Praised, bowed to and honoured, they are the highest in the world, the perfected ones. May they grant health of the soul, the gain of right understanding, and the best, highest calm.

v.7 · Moon, sun and ocean

चंदेसु णिम्मलयरा, आइच्चेसु अहियं पयासयरा ।
सागरवरगंभीरा, सिद्धा सिद्धिं मम दिसंतु ॥७॥

Purer than the moons, shining more than the suns, deep as the greatest ocean: may the Siddhas grant me perfection.

Preface (p. 86, from the Uttaradhyayan Sutra) · What the praise gives

चउव्वीसत्थएणं भंते ! जीवे किं जणयइ ?
चउव्वीसत्थएणं दंसण विसोहिं जणयइ ।

“Venerable one, what does the soul gain by praising the twenty-four?” “By praising the twenty-four, it purifies its faith.”

Commentary (p. 95, from the Avashyak Niryukti) · Devotion brings its own reward

भत्तीइ जिनवराणं परमाए खीणपिज्जदोसाणं ।
आरुग्ग बोहिलाभं समाहि मरणं पावंति ॥१०९७॥

By supreme devotion to the Jinas, whose attachment and aversion are destroyed, devotees gain health of the soul, right understanding, and a calm death.

The translation numbers this Niryukti verse 1017; the print reads १०९७. The print also has जिनवराणं (with न) here, where the Logassa uses जिणवरा.
