Uttaradhyayan 36 · Jivajiva-Vibhakti: The Living and the Non-living

In one line: the whole world is made of the living and the non-living; the chapter maps every kind of each, from atoms to the siddhas, so that a monk who knows and trusts this map delights in restraint, dies well, and reaches liberation.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra Part 2, chapter 36 (274 verses, the last chapter of the sutra), translated pages 418–505. 25 minutes.

What the chapter teaches

  1. The world and the non-world. Where living and non-living things exist is the world (loka); beyond it, where there is only space, is the non-world (aloka). Both have no beginning and no end, so no creator, keeper or destroyer. Everything is described by substance, place, time and state. Knowing the difference between living and non-living is the reason for the chapter: “knowing it, a monk strives rightly in restraint” (v.1–3).
  2. The non-living (ajiva): 14 kinds. Formless are the medium of motion (dharmastikaya, which helps beings and matter move, as water helps a fish), the medium of rest (adharmastikaya, like shade for a tired traveller), and space (akasha), each counted as whole, part and smallest point, plus time: 10 kinds. The first two fill the world, space fills world and non-world, time is counted only in the human region. Matter (pudgala) alone has form: aggregate, part, point and atom, 4 kinds (v.4–10).
  3. Matter and its 530 modes. Atoms join into aggregates and aggregates break into atoms. Matter has 5 colours, 2 smells, 5 tastes, 8 touches and 5 shapes; because these always occur together, their combinations give the 530 modes the commentary counts out (v.11–48).
  4. The liberated (siddhas). Living beings are worldly or liberated. Liberation can come in a female, male or neuter body; in a monk’s own dress, another dress, or a householder’s; from above, below or the middle world, even from the ocean. The verses give how many can be liberated in one instant, for example 108 men, 20 women, 4 householders. Liberated souls are stopped by the non-world and rest at the top of the world, above Siddhashila: an umbrella-shaped earth of white gold, 45 lakh yojanas wide, 8 yojanas thick at the centre and thinner than a fly’s wing at the edge. There they are formless, all knowledge and perception, in a happiness that has no comparison (v.49–68).
  5. Worldly beings: the immobile and the “moving” elements. Worldly beings are mobile or immobile. Earth, water and plant beings are immobile; fire and air are counted with the mobile because they move. Each kind is subtle (filling the whole world) or gross (in one part), developed or undeveloped. Gross earth includes soft clays and 36 hard kinds, from sand and salt to gold, diamond and gems. Plants are either one soul per body (trees, creepers, grasses) or many souls sharing one body (potato, radish, ginger, onion, garlic and other root plants). Fire includes embers, flames, lightning; air includes whirlwinds and pure wind (v.69–126).
  6. Beings with two, three and four senses. Two senses: worms, earthworms, shells, conches, cowries, leeches. Three: ants, termites, lice and other small insects. Four: flies, mosquitoes, bees, moths, scorpions (most four-sensed names are unclear in the source, v.147–149). All live in only part of the world (v.127–155).
  7. Five-sensed beings: hell, animals, humans, gods. Hell beings live in seven earths, from Ratnaprabha to Tamastamahprabha. Animals are born of a womb or spontaneously, and live in water (fish, turtles, crocodiles), on land (one-hoofed, two-hoofed, round-footed and clawed four-footed beasts, and creatures that crawl on limbs or on the belly), or in the air (birds with leathery or feathered wings, and two kinds not seen here). Humans live in the 15 lands of action, 30 lands without action and 56 islands between. Gods are of four classes: 10 kinds of mansion gods, 8 kinds of forest gods, 5 kinds of luminaries (moons, suns, lunar mansions, planets, stars), and the heavenly gods of the 12 heavens, 9 Graiveyakas and 5 Anuttara abodes, the highest being Sarvarthasiddha (v.156–253).
  8. Every kind has its clock. For each group the chapter states the lifespan, how long a soul can keep being reborn in that same group, and the gap before it returns. Each group also has “thousands of varieties” by colour, smell, taste, touch and shape. The maximum lifespans, as the verses give them:

  1. From knowing to restraint, and a good death. Having heard and believed this account, the monk should delight in restraint. After many years of monk’s life he thins body and passions by sallekhana: at best 12 years (four years without rich foods, four of varied fasts, then alternate-day fasts with ayambil, and a final year of ayambil before giving up food), at middle one year, at least six months (v.254–261).
  2. What spoils the end. Five dispositions lead to a low birth even among gods: frivolous joking and lewd talk (kandarpa), using mantras and charms for comfort (abhiyogiki), deceitfully slandering knowledge, the omniscient, the teacher and the community (kilvishi), nursing anger (asuri), and suicide or gross misconduct (moha). Those who die in wrong belief, with wishes for reward (nidana), violence or the black leshya find awakening (bodhi) hard to regain; those who die in right belief, without nidana and in the white leshya find it easily. Those devoted to the Jina’s word have few births left. A confession should be made to a monk who knows the Agamas, brings peace and sees virtue (v.262–273).
  3. The close of the whole sutra. Thus the enlightened Lord Mahavira, son of the Jnata clan, made known these thirty-six chapters, prized by those fit for liberation (v.274).

Key verses

2 · World and non-world

जीवा चेव अजीवा य, एस लोए वियाहिए ।
अजीव देसमागासे, अलोए से वियाहिए ॥

This world is said to consist of the living and the non-living. Where there is only space, a part of the non-living, that is called the non-world.

57 · Where the liberated rest

अलोए पडिहया सिद्धा, लोयग्गे य पइट्ठिया ।
इहं बोंदिं चइत्ताणं, तत्थ गंतूण सिज्झइ ॥

The liberated are stopped by the non-world and established at the top of the world. Leaving the body here, the soul goes there and attains liberation.

255 · Knowing leads to restraint

इय जीवमजीवे य, सोच्चा सद्दहिऊण य ।
सव्व णयाणमणुमए, रमेज्ज संजमे मुणी ॥

Having thus heard about the living and the non-living and believed it, the monk should delight in restraint, which is approved from every point of view.

264 · Dying in the light

सम्मदंसणरत्ता, अणियाणा सुक्कलेसमोगाढा ।
इय जे मरंति जीवा, तेसिं सुलहा भवे बोही ॥

Those who die devoted to right belief, free of wishes for reward and steeped in the white leshya will find awakening easy to attain.

274 · The end of the sutra

इइ पाउकरे बुद्धे, णायए परिणिव्वुए ॥
छत्तीसं उत्तरज्झाए, भवसिद्धिय सम्मए ॥ त्ति बेमि ॥

Thus the enlightened one of the Jnata clan, who reached nirvana, made known the thirty-six chapters of the Uttaradhyayan, held dear by souls fit for liberation. Thus I say.
