Avashyak 1 · Samayik (પ્રથમ આવશ્યક : સામાયિક): Equanimity

In one line: the first daily duty is equanimity: bow to the five supreme beings, then give up all sinful activity in mind, speech and body, turn back from past wrongs, and drop the self that did them.

Source: Avashyak Sutra, 1st Avashyak (2 lessons, 1 sutra each: the Namaskar Mantra and the Samayik pledge Karemi Bhante), translated pages 65–85 (data/translations/avashyak/). Pages 65–71 are the book’s introduction to all six Avashyaks; the chapter text proper starts on page 72. References read L(esson).sutra, with page numbers for the introduction and commentary. 12 minutes.

What the chapter teaches

  1. An Avashyak is a duty that must be done every day and night. There are six: equanimity (Samayik), praise of the 24 Tirthankaras, bowing to the Guru, turning back from faults (Pratikraman), standing still and letting go of the body (Kayotsarga), and renouncing (Pratyakhyan). They are binding on monks, nuns, laymen and laywomen alike, because their work is the soul’s own cleansing, not the body’s needs (pp. 67–68).
  2. Done without attention, it does not count. Reciting the texts by rote and going through the standing and sitting with the mind elsewhere is only the outer form (dravya avashyak). The real thing (bhava avashyak) is done with full attention, seeking no fame or reward, the mind on the meaning, morning and evening (p. 69).
  3. Each duty has its own purpose, and the order is natural. Samayik is giving up sinful activity; the praise of the 24 holds up those who perfected it; bowing honours the virtuous; Pratikraman censures lapses; Kayotsarga heals the wounds of faults; Pratyakhyan holds the virtues gained. The order follows the seeker’s growth: he settles into equanimity, admires those who perfected it, bows to the Guru, repents, purifies himself in meditation, and only then is fit to renounce further (pp. 69–71).
  4. The chapter opens with the Namaskar Mantra, which bows to virtues, not to persons. No individual is named. Arihant, Siddha, Acharya, Upadhyaya and Sadhu are titles a soul earns; the path itself runs upward from Sadhu to Siddha. The word namo means bowing the five limbs outwardly and giving up pride inwardly (L1.1; pp. 72–75).
  5. Arihants come before Siddhas because they teach. Siddhas are higher, but we know of them only through the Arihants’ teaching, and the Arihant, still embodied, shows the path. Arihant and Siddha stand for God, the other three for the Guru (pp. 75–76).
  6. The closing four lines state the fruit. “This fivefold bow destroys all sin and is the first of all auspicious things.” The book notes that the old commentaries explain only the first five lines; some Acharyas treat these four as an appendix (chulika). The commentary lists eight fruits, from health and wealth in this life to heaven, a good birth and finally liberation (pp. 76–77).
  7. Real auspiciousness is inner. Curd, rice and the swastika are outward luck charms that can turn unlucky; bowing, praise and the three jewels never turn bad. The practitioner begins by bowing to the five as someone separate, and ends by becoming what he bows to (pp. 77–78).
  8. The 108 virtues are a tradition, not an Agama count. Arihant 12 + Siddha 8 + Acharya 36 + Upadhyaya 25 + Sadhu 27 = 108. The book says plainly that the Agamas give no count for Arihant, Acharya or Upadhyaya; only the Siddha’s 31 virtues and the Sadhu’s 27 appear in the Agamas (pp. 78–81).
  9. The Samayik pledge is taken freely, before a witness. Karemi, “I undertake”, shows it is the disciple’s own choice. Bhante is the Guru, or God when no Guru is present, or one’s own soul, whose witness matters most. Every explanation of samayik comes to one word: equanimity (L2.1; pp. 81–84).
  10. A monk takes it for life, a householder for a set time. The monk renounces sin in nine ways (doing, causing, approving × mind, speech, body). A householder says jav niyamam, “for as long as the rule lasts”, fixed by tradition at 48 minutes per Samayik, and cannot renounce approval because his business and kitchen run on in his name; so he vows in six ways (some Acharyas say eight) (pp. 84–85).
  11. Turn back, censure, confess, let go. Nindami is censuring one’s own fault inwardly; garihami is confessing it before the Guru, a harder practice because it breaks one’s pride; appanam vosirami gives up not the soul but the self of passions and wrong activity (p. 85).

Key passages

L1.1 · The Namaskar Mantra

णमो अरिहंताणं
णमो सिद्धाणं
णमो आयरियाणं
णमो उवज्झायाणं
णमो लोए सव्वसाहूणं

I bow to the Arihants. I bow to the Siddhas. I bow to the Acharyas. I bow to the Upadhyayas. I bow to all the Sadhus in the world.

Chulika (p. 76) · The first of all auspicious things

एसो पंच णमोक्कारो सव्व पावप्पणासणो ।
मंगलाणं च सव्वेसिं पढमं हवइ मंगलं ॥

This fivefold bow destroys all sins, and of all auspicious things it is the first. (The book treats these lines as an appendix that the old commentaries do not explain.)

L2.1 · The Samayik pledge (Karemi Bhante)

करेमि भंते ! सामाइयं सावज्जं जोगं पच्चक्खामि जावज्जीवाए तिविहं तिविहेणं मणेणं वायाए काएणं ण करेमि ण कारवेमि करतंपि अण्णं ण समणुजाणामि तस्स भंते पडिक्कमामि णिंदामि गरिहामि अप्पाणं वोसिरामि ।

Venerable one, I undertake Samayik. I renounce sinful activity for as long as I live, in three ways and by three means: by mind, speech and body I will not do it, will not make others do it, and will not approve of another doing it. From that, venerable one, I turn back; I censure it before my own soul, I confess it before the Guru, and I give up that sinful self.

The print here reads करतंपि; the same pledge in the 4th Avashyak (p. 109) prints करंतं पि.

Introduction (p. 67) · Why it is called Avashyak

समणेण सावएण य, अवस्स कायव्वं हवइ जम्हा ।
अंतो अहो-निसस्स उ, तम्हा आवस्सयं णामं ॥

Because the monk and the householder must necessarily do it within each day and night, it is called Avashyak.

Introduction (p. 69, from the Anuyogadvara Sutra) · The six aims

सावज्जजोगविरई, उक्कित्तण गुणवओ य पडिवत्ती ।
खलियस्स णिंदणा वणतिगिच्छ गुणधारणा चेव ॥१॥

Giving up sinful activity, praise, honour to the virtuous, censure of lapses, healing of wounds, and holding fast to virtues: these are the six aims of the six Avashyaks.

Commentary (p. 83, from the Avashyak Bhashya) · The soul itself is Samayik

सावज्ज जोग विरओ, तिगुत्तो छसु संजओ उवउत्तो जयमाणो आया सामाईयं होइ ॥

When the soul has turned away from sinful activity, is guarded in mind, speech and body, restrained toward the six kinds of living beings, attentive and careful, that soul itself is Samayik.
