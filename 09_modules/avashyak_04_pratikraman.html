Avashyak 4 · Pratikraman (ચોથો આવશ્યક : પ્રતિક્રમણ): Turning Back

In one line: each day the monk looks back over everything he did, waking and sleeping, walking, begging and studying, turns back from every lapse with “may that wrong of mine be undone”, renews his faith and vows, and ends by forgiving and asking forgiveness of every living being.

Source: Avashyak Sutra, 4th Avashyak (10 lessons, 62 numbered sutras; the 4th Shramana Sutra alone has 45), translated pages 106–191 (data/translations/avashyak/). The brief gave 106–187, but the chapter runs on to the forgiveness sutra and ends on p. 191 (“Avashyak-4 complete”). References read L(esson).sutra. 25 minutes.

What the chapter teaches

  1. Ten lessons, and no vows recited. The chapter has: Karemi Bhante, the four auspicious things, the short Pratikraman, the walking sutra, five Shramana Sutras and the forgiveness sutra. The book notes that the Avashyak Sutra itself has no recital of the monk’s five great vows or the householder’s twelve vows; the present custom of adding them before the Shramana Sutras is tradition (pp. 106–108).
  2. First, take refuge. The pledge is repeated (now with savvam, “all” sinful activity), then four things are declared auspicious, highest and the only refuge: the Arihants, the Siddhas, the Sadhus and the dharma taught by the omniscient. Coconut and kumkum are not real auspiciousness; no person or thing in the world can shelter the soul, but dharma is an island for one drowning in the sea of births (L1–L2; pp. 109–113).
  3. The short Pratikraman lists every area of a monk’s life. Body, speech and mind; going against scripture or the path; the three restraints, four passions, five great vows, six kinds of beings, seven ways of seeking alms, eight “mothers of the teaching”, nine fences of celibacy, ten monk’s virtues. A vow partly broken or badly damaged is cleansed by Pratikraman; a vow wholly broken (anachara) needs the Guru’s penance (L3; pp. 113–118).
  4. Harm means much more than killing. The walking sutra (Iriyavahiyam) names ten kinds of harm, from striking a creature in one’s way, covering it, crushing it and frightening it, to moving it from its place and taking its life. Violence is injuring any of the ten life-forces through careless or passionate activity; a surgeon with a pure intention is not violent. The Niryukti reads michchhami dukkadam letter by letter: humility, covering one’s faults, staying within the limits, despising the wrong in oneself, admitting it, and calming it away (L4; pp. 118–123).
  5. Faults in sleep and dreams count too. Sleeping too long or on too soft a bed, turning over without first brushing the bed, yawning without care, and in dreams being stirred by battles and weddings or wanting food at night: the monk is answerable for all of it, and reflects on it on waking (L5; pp. 123–126).
  6. Begging must not burden anyone. The monk takes a little from many houses, like a cow grazing only the tips of the grass or a bee taking nectar without hurting the flower. He turns back from pushing open a closed door, brushing past dogs, calves and children, taking food in doubt or in haste, or food mixed with living seeds or water. Food found impure afterwards must be given up, not eaten (L6; pp. 126–131).
  7. Study and inspection at their proper times. Four periods of study a day and night, inspection of clothes and bowls morning and evening, and careful brushing of any small creature to safety. The commentary’s example of the four grades of fault: accepting an invitation to food cooked for the monk is the first (atikrama), setting out to fetch it the second, taking it the third, eating it the fourth (L7; pp. 131–135).
  8. One to thirty-three: what to know, what to drop, what to practise. The long 4th Shramana Sutra counts from one kind of non-restraint, two bonds (attachment and aversion, “the two seeds of karma”), three thorns (deceit, longing for reward, false belief), four passions (conquer anger with forgiveness, pride with gentleness, deceit with straightness, greed with contentment), up to eight prides, nine fences of celibacy, the 22 hardships and the 33 disrespects. The book’s own table marks each as to be known, given up or adopted (L8.1–45; pp. 135–175).
  9. Disrespect reaches everyone. The second list of 33 disrespects includes householders, men and women, gods, this world and the next, and “all living beings”; mispronouncing or misordering the scripture, or studying it at the wrong time, is disrespect to knowledge. The commentary says monks and Acharyas too must ask pardon each day for any disrespect to laymen and laywomen (L8.45; pp. 168–173).
  10. Then the vow is renewed. The 5th Shramana Sutra praises the teaching of the Nirgranthas as true, complete and the path that ends all sorrow; those who stand in it become perfect. The monk declares faith in it, then gives up eight things and takes their opposites: non-restraint for restraint, unchastity for celibacy, the improper for the proper, ignorance for knowledge, wrong action for right action, false belief for right belief, unawareness for awakening, the wrong path for the right. He turns back from faults remembered and forgotten, and reminds himself: “I am a Shramana.” He bows to every monk in the fifteen lands where dharma can be practised, keepers of the 18,000 limbs of conduct (L9.1–7; pp. 175–190).
  11. It ends in forgiveness. He forgives all beings and asks their forgiveness, declares friendship with all and enmity with none, and bows to the 24 Jinas. Only one who feels friendship toward every living being can become free of attachment (L10.1; pp. 190–191).

Key passages

L2.1 and L2.3 · The four auspicious things, the four refuges

चत्तारि मंगलं-अरिहंता मंगलं, सिद्धा मंगलं, साहू मंगलं, केवलि-पण्णतो धम्मो मंगलं ।

चत्तारि सरणं पवज्जामि- अरिहंते सरणं पवज्जामि, सिद्धे सरणं पवज्जामि, साहू सरणं पवज्जामि, केवलि पण्णत्तं धम्मं सरणं पवज्जामि ।

Four are auspicious: the Arihants, the Siddhas, the Sadhus, and the dharma taught by the omniscient. I take four refuges: I take refuge in the Arihants, in the Siddhas, in the Sadhus, and in the dharma taught by the omniscient.

L4.1 · Turning back from harm done while walking

इच्छामि पडिक्कमिउं इरियावहियाए विराहणाए गमणागमणे पाणक्कमणे बीयक्कमणे हरियक्कमणे ओसा उत्तिंग पणग दग मट्टी मक्कडा संताणा संकमणे जे मे जीवा विराहिया एगिंदिया बेइंदिया तेइंदिया चउरिंदिया पंचिंदिया अभिहया वत्तिया लेसिया संघाइया संघट्टिया परियाविया किलामिया उद्दविया ठाणाओठाणं संक्कामिया जीवियाओ ववरोविया तस्स मिच्छामि दुक्कडम् ।

I wish to turn back from harm done on the path. In going and coming, treading on living creatures, seeds or green plants, on dew, ant-holes, moss, water, wet earth or spiders’ webs: whatever beings I have harmed, with one, two, three, four or five senses, if I struck them, covered them, crushed them, heaped them together, jostled them, tormented them, wearied them, terrified them, moved them from place to place or took their life, may that wrong of mine be undone.

The print writes इरियावहियाए with an older form of the final ए; the word meanings below it spell it इरियावहियाए. It prints संक्कामिया where the translation has संकामिया.

L9.2 · The teaching that ends all sorrow

इणमेव णिग्गंथं पावयणं सच्चं अणुतरं केवलियं पडिपुण्णं णेआउयं संसुद्धं सल्लकत्तणं सिद्धिमग्गं मुत्तिमग्गं णिज्जाणमग्गं णिव्वाणमग्गं अवितहमविसंधि सव्वदुक्खप्पहीणमग्गं ।

This very teaching of the Nirgranthas is true, unsurpassed, taught by the omniscient, complete, leading to liberation, wholly pure, the cutter of the inner thorns, the path to perfection, to release, to leaving the round of births and to nirvana; it is never false, never broken, the path that ends all sorrow.

The print reads अणुतरं (single त) in both the sutra and its word meanings; the translation has अणुत्तरं. The print reads सल्लकत्तणं; the translation has सल्लकरणं.

L9.6 · “I am a Shramana”

समणोऽहं संजय विरय पडिहय पच्चक्खाय पावकम्मो अणियाणो दिट्ठि संपण्णो मायामोसो विवज्जिओ ।

I am a Shramana: restrained, turned away from sin, one who has struck down and renounced sinful karma, free of longing for reward, possessed of right vision, and free of deceitful falsehood.

L10.1 · Forgiveness and friendship

खामेमि सव्वे जीवा, सव्वे जीवा वि खमंतु मे ।
मित्तीमे सव्व भूएसू, वेरं मज्झं ण केणइ ॥१॥
एवमहं आलोइय, णिंदिय गरिहिय दुगंछियं सम्मं ।
तिविहेण पडिक्कंतो, वंदामि जिणे चउव्वीसं ॥२॥

I forgive all living beings; may all living beings forgive me. I have friendship with all beings and enmity with none. Having thus rightly reviewed my faults, censured them within, confessed them before the Guru and turned from them in disgust, and having turned back in all three ways, I bow to the twenty-four Jinas.
