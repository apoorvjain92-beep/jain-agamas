Acharang 1.5 · Lokasara (લોકસાર): Essence of the World

In one line: what is truly worth having in this world is not pleasure or possessions but dharma, knowledge, restraint and finally liberation, so fight the enemy within, live close to the guru, trust the Jina’s word, and see every other being as yourself.

Source: Acharang Sutra, Book 1 (First Shrutaskandha), chapter 5 (41 sutras, 6 sections), translated pages 227–281 (data/translations/acharang-1/). The book numbers the sutras afresh in each section, so references below read section.sutra (5.3.4 = section 3, sutra 4). The chapter is also called “Avanti”, from the first word of sections 1–3. 16 minutes.

What the chapter teaches

  1. The essence of the world is dharma, and the essence of restraint is liberation. The introduction quotes the Niryukti: dharma is the essence of the world, knowledge the essence of dharma, restraint the essence of knowledge, and nirvana the essence of restraint. Pleasures, wealth and the body are without essence (introduction, pp.227–228).
  2. Pleasures are hard to drop, and life is a dewdrop on a blade of grass. Whoever harms beings, with a purpose or without one, is born among those same beings. Because pleasures are heavy to give up, one stays inside death’s reach and far from liberation. The fool’s life is as unsteady as a drop of water on a blade of kusa grass shaken by the wind (5.1.1–3).
  3. Guard celibacy, and do not hide a fault. One who understands what unsettles celibacy can get free of worldly life. A monk who breaks it and then hides it from the guru commits a second folly. The section also warns against the false lone monk who is full of anger, pride, deceit and greed and says “let no one see me”, and against those who teach that ignorance frees (5.1.4–7).
  4. This human body is the moment; do not waste it. One who lives without harming sees “this is the opportunity” and “this is the moment for this body”. When illness comes, bear it calmly: the body breaks, does not last and changes all the time. Possessions, small or large, living or not, become a great fear. Bondage and liberation lie in one’s own inner state (5.2.1–5).
  5. Fight yourself, not others. The one free of possessions should not hide his strength. Some rise and never fall, some rise and fall, some never rise. Fight the passions within, for a chance to fight that battle is rare. The one who falls is caught by forms and by violence. What is right is monkhood, and monkhood is what is right; the lax, the crooked and the home-minded cannot keep it (5.3.1–7).
  6. A young monk should not wander alone; he should live near the guru. An immature monk alone is easily angered, proud and overcome by hardship. Instead he should see with the guru’s eyes, rest his mind in the guru, put the guru first and live near him. Walking with care, a monk who harms a creature by accident binds only light karma and cleans it by atonement (5.4.1–4).
  7. When desire troubles a monk, the book gives remedies. In order: eat plain food, eat less, stand in meditation, travel from village to village, and give up food. Turn the mind away from women; do not talk of them, stare at them or seek closeness. The commentary says the same teaching applies to nuns with respect to men (5.4.5–7).
  8. The great teacher is like a full lake, and the Jina’s word is beyond doubt. A mind full of doubt finds no peace. That alone is true and beyond doubt which the Jinas taught; differences among monks come from their own karma, not from the teaching. Right thinking makes even hard things right; wrong thinking spoils even right ones (5.5.1–5).
  9. The one you would harm is yourself. The one you think fit to kill, rule, torment, enslave or trouble is you. So the straightforward, awakened one neither kills nor makes others kill. The self is the knower, and the knower is the self (5.5.6–8).
  10. Test every teaching by the Agama, and know the liberated soul. Some act against the command, some fail to act on it; stay near the guru. A tested monk may live without support. Streams of karmic inflow run above, below and across; close them and cross the round of birth and death. The liberated soul has no shape, colour, smell, taste, touch, body or sex; words turn back and reason cannot reach it (5.6.1–7).

Key passages

5.2.4 · Bondage and freedom lie within

से सुयं च मे अज्झत्थयं च मे, बंधपमोक्खो अज्झत्थेव ।

I have heard it, and I have reflected on it and experienced it myself: bondage and release come from within, from the soul’s own inner states.

5.3.4 · Fight the battle within

इमेण चेव जुज्झाहि, किं ते जुज्झेण बज्झओ ? जुद्धारिहं खलु दुल्लहं । जहेत्थ कुसलेहिं परिण्णाविवेगे भासिए ।

Fight with this very self. What good to you is fighting outside enemies? What is fit for this battle is truly hard to find. For it the wise ones have taught knowing and letting go: understanding the enemy, and separating yourself from it.

5.5.2 · That alone is true

वितिगिच्छासमावण्णेणं अप्पाणेणं णो लहइ समाहिं । सिया वेगे अणुगच्छंति, असिया वेगे अणुगच्छंति । अणुगच्छमाणेहिं अणणुगच्छमाणे कहं ण णिव्विज्जे ? तमेव सच्चं णीसंकं जं जिणेहिं पवेइयं ।

A soul caught in doubt does not find inner peace (samadhi). Some follow the command, some do not. How could one who does not follow it not be disheartened by those who do? That alone is true and beyond doubt which the Jinas have taught.

5.5.6 · You are the one

तुमं सि णाम सच्चेव जं हंतव्वं ति मण्णसि । तुमं सि णाम सच्चेव जं अज्जावेयव्वं ति मण्णसि । तुमं सि णाम सच्चेव जं परियावेयव्वं ति मण्णसि । तुमं सि णाम सच्चेव जं परिघेयव्वं ति मण्णसि । तुमं सि णाम सच्चेव जं उद्दवेयव्वं ति मण्णसि ।

The one you think fit to kill is you yourself. The one you think fit to rule over is you. The one you think fit to torment is you. The one you think fit to seize and enslave is you. The one you think fit to trouble to death is you.

5.5.8 · The self is the knower

जे आया से विण्णाया, जे विण्णाया से आया । जेण वियाणइ से आया । तं पडुच्च पडिसंखाए । एस आयावाई समियाए परियाए वियाहिए । त्ति बेमि ।

The self is the knower, and the knower is the self. That by which one knows is the self. One should reflect on the self in this light. Such a one is called a true believer in the self, and his restraint is right. Thus I say.

5.6.7 · Where words turn back (opening lines)

सव्वे सरा णियट्टंति, तक्का जत्थ ण विज्जइ, मई तत्थ ण गाहिया । ओए अप्पइट्ठाणस्स खेयण्णे ।
से ण दीहे, ण हस्से, ण वट्टे, ण तंसे, ण चउरंसे, ण परिमंडले, ण किण्हे, ण णीले, ण लोहिए, ण हालिद्दे, ण सुक्किले, ण सुब्भिगंधे, ण दुब्भिगंधे, ण तित्ते, ण कडुए, ण कसाए, ण अंबिले, ण महुरे, ण कक्खडे, ण मउए, ण गरुए, ण लहुए ण सीए, ण उण्हे, ण णिद्धे, ण लुक्खे, ण काऊ ण रुहे, ण संगे, ण इत्थी, ण पुरिसे, ण अण्णहा ।

All words turn back from it; reason does not reach it; the mind cannot grasp it. Alone and free of the stain of karma, it knows the state beyond the world. It is not long or short, round, triangular, square or ring-shaped; not black, green, red, yellow or white; not sweet-smelling or foul-smelling; not pungent, bitter, astringent, sour or sweet; not hard or soft, heavy or light, cold or hot, smooth or rough. It has no body, is not born again, has no tie to karma, and is neither female, male nor other. (The print has no “not salty” in the sutra, though the word meanings list ण लवणे; the print is kept as it stands.)
