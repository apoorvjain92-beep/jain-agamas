Uttaradhyayan 29 · Samyak Parakram (Sammatta-parakkame): Right Effort

In one line: for each of 73 practices, from longing for liberation to the final stillness, the disciple asks “Lord, what does the soul gain by this?”, and each answer shows how that one practice, done rightly, carries the soul all the way to freedom.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra Part 2, chapter 29 (76 prose sutras: an opening, a list of 73 topics, one question and answer for each of the 73, and a closing), translated pages 212–277 (data/translations/uttaradhyayan-2/). 35 minutes.

How the chapter works. Almost every sutra has the same shape. ”___-eṇaṃ bhante! jīve kiṃ jaṇayai?”: “Lord, what does the soul gain through ___?” Then comes the answer. Effort turned outward, toward possessions and success, is not right effort; effort turned inward, toward purifying the soul, is (commentary, s.1). The introduction says any one practice can be a starting point. Each topic stands on its own, and all of them lead to the soul’s purity. Sutra numbers below are the book’s: the first topic is s.3, so topic n is sutra n+2.

What the chapter teaches

  1. Ten steps turn a teaching into freedom. Sudharma tells Jambu that Mahavira taught this chapter. Whoever, in order, trusts it, is convinced of it, comes to like it, takes it up, keeps it, sees it through, praises it, purifies it, practises it and follows it as commanded becomes perfected and ends all suffering. Sutra 2 then lists the 73 topics (s.1–2).
  2. It begins with longing, letting go and trust. Longing for liberation (samvega) brings the highest faith, and that faith brings longing back quickly. Such a soul destroys the worst passions, the ones that bind without end; it may be freed in this very life and will not need more than three lives. Weariness with the world (nirveda) turns the soul from heavenly, human and animal pleasures and cuts off the road of rebirth. Faith in dharma loosens the hold of comfort; the soul leaves household life and gains unobstructed happiness. Serving the guru and fellow followers brings humility, which closes off bad births. Confession pulls out three thorns: deceit, wanting rewards for one’s practice, and wrong belief. Self-reproach brings remorse, and that remorse leads the soul up toward destroying deluding karma. Confessing before others breaks pride and turns the soul to wholesome activity (s.3–9).
  3. The daily essentials each have a fruit. Equanimity (samayika) stops sinful activity. Praising the 24 Tirthankaras purifies faith. Bowing to the guru removes low-status karma and brings goodwill. Pratikramana mends the holes in one’s vows. Kayotsarga lifts off faults “like a porter who has put down his load”. Taking a vow of renunciation (pratyakhyana) shuts the doors through which karma flows in and quiets desire. Hymns of praise bring awakening in knowledge, faith and conduct. Keeping the right times for study removes knowledge-obscuring karma. Atonement cleans sinful karma. Asking forgiveness brings gladness, friendship with all beings and fearlessness (s.10–19).
  4. Study has five parts, and each one helps. Self-study (svadhyaya) destroys knowledge-obscuring karma. Then come its five parts. Reading and teaching keep the scriptures alive and shed karma. Asking questions clears doubts. Repeating lessons keeps the words in memory. Deep reflection (anupreksha) loosens all karma except lifespan karma, and makes it shorter, milder and lighter. Teaching dharma to others spreads the faith. Honouring scripture ends ignorance, and fixing the mind on one point stills its wandering (s.20–27).
  5. Restraint stops new karma, austerity burns the old, and the last purification ends activity. Restraint (samyama) makes one free of sin; austerity (tapa) purifies; full purification (vodana) brings the inactive state and then liberation. The commentary calls these three successive steps. Turning away from pleasure makes one gentle and sorrowless; non-attachment lets one move “alone, one-pointed, day and night”. Secluded lodging guards conduct, and turning back from sense objects carries one across the forest of rebirth (s.28–34).
  6. The renunciations go deeper one by one. Eating apart from the group makes a monk content with his own alms. Giving up equipment frees time for study. Giving up food cuts the craving to live, and giving up the passions makes pleasure and pain equal. Giving up the activity of mind, speech and body stops all new bondage. Giving up the body brings the qualities of the perfected ones at the top of the universe. Giving up help from others brings few quarrels and much calm. A lifelong fast cuts off many hundreds of births. The final renunciation, of all activity in the fourteenth stage, destroys the last four karmas (s.35–43). The commentary notes that possessions, food, passions, activity and body are given up in that order.
  7. Outer form and inner virtue go together. Wearing the true monk’s marks makes one light, careful and trusted by all beings. Service (vaiyavritya) binds the karma of a future Tirthankara. Having all the virtues means never returning to suffering. Freedom from attachment cuts the bonds of affection and craving. Forgiveness conquers hardships, and freedom from greed leaves one owning nothing, of no interest to the greedy. Straightforwardness makes body, mind and speech honest, and gentleness destroys the eight kinds of pride (s.44–51).
  8. Truth, guarding and steadying lead to the three jewels made full. Truth in thought, action and activity purifies the soul. A truthful soul “does as it says”. Guarding the mind gives one-pointedness, guarding speech gives a mind free of agitation, and guarding the body stops sinful inflow. Steadying the mind purifies faith; steadying speech makes awakening easy to reach again in later lives; steadying the body leads to perfect conduct and liberation. Fullness of knowledge keeps the soul from getting lost, like a threaded needle. Fullness of faith keeps its light from going out. Fullness of conduct brings the rock-still state (shaileshi) (s.52–63).
  9. Master the five senses, then the four passions. Restraining hearing, sight, smell, taste and touch stops attachment and aversion toward pleasant and unpleasant sounds, forms, smells, tastes and touches; no new karma is bound and old karma falls away. Conquering anger brings forgiveness, pride brings softness, deceit brings straightforwardness, and greed brings contentment. Conquering attachment, aversion and wrong belief destroys deluding karma and then the other destructive karmas, and brings omniscience. While still active, the omniscient one binds only a karma that lasts two instants and is shed in the third (s.64–73).
  10. The end is stillness and an upward flight. With less than a muhurta of life left, the omniscient one stops mind, then speech, then body, then breath. In the time it takes to say five short vowels (a, i, u, ṛ, ḷ) he destroys the last four karmas. Leaving all three bodies, the soul rises straight up, in a single instant and without turning, to the top of the universe and is perfected (s.74–75). Sudharma closes: this is the meaning of the chapter as Mahavira explained it (s.76).

Key passages

s.3 · Longing for liberation, the first step

संवेगेणं भंते ! जीवे किं जणयइ ?
संवेगेणं अणुत्तरं धम्मसद्धं जणयइ, अणुत्तराए धम्मसद्धाए संवेगं हव्वमागच्छइ, अणंताणुबंधी कोह-माण-माया-लोभे खवेइ, णवं च कम्मं ण बंधइ, तप्पच्चइयं च मिच्छत्त-विसोहिं काऊण दंसणाराहए भवइ, दंसणविसोहिए य णं विसुद्धाए अत्थेगइए जीवे तेणेव भवग्गहणेणं सिज्झइ बुज्झइ मुच्चइ परिणिव्वायइ सव्वदुक्खाणमंतं करेइ, विसोहिए य णं विसुद्धाए तच्चं पुणो भवग्गहणं णाइक्कमइ ।

Lord, what does the soul gain through longing for liberation? It gains the highest faith in dharma, and through that faith the longing quickly grows. It destroys the anger, pride, deceit and greed that bind without end, and binds no new karma. Clearing away wrong belief, it becomes a true keeper of faith. Purified by that faith, some souls are perfected in this very life. Even the rest do not go beyond a third birth.

s.19 · Asking forgiveness

खमावणयाए णं भंते ! जीवे किं जणयइ ?
खमावणयाए णं पल्हायणभावं जणयइ । पल्हायण भावमुवगए य सव्वपाण-भूय-जीव-सत्तेसु मित्तीभावमुप्पाएइ । मित्तीभावमुवगए यावि जीवे भावविसोहिं काऊण णिब्भए भवइ ।

Lord, what does the soul gain by asking forgiveness? It gains gladness of heart. Glad at heart, it feels friendship for all living beings. In friendship, it purifies its feelings and becomes fearless.

s.20 · Self-study

सज्झाएणं भंते ! जीवे किं जणयइ ? सज्झाएणं णाणावरणिज्जं कम्मं खवेइ ।

Lord, what does the soul gain through self-study? Through self-study it destroys the karma that covers knowledge.

s.61 · The threaded needle

जहा सूई ससुत्ता, पडिया वि ण विणस्सइ ।
तहा जीवे ससुत्ते, संसारे ण विणस्सइ ॥

A needle with its thread is not lost, even when it falls. In the same way, a soul that holds the scripture is not lost in the world.

s.75 · The flight to the top of the universe

तओ ओरालिय-तेयकम्माइं सव्वाहिं विप्पजहणाहिं विप्पजहित्ता उज्जुसेढिपत्ते अफुसमाणगईए उड्ढं एगसमएणं अविग्गहेणं तत्थ गंता सागारोवउत्ते सिज्झइ बुज्झइ मुच्चइ परिणिव्वायइ सव्वादुक्खाणमंतं करेइ ।

Then, casting off completely the physical, fiery and karmic bodies, the soul takes the straight path. Without touching anything on the way, it rises in a single instant, without turning, and goes there. Fully aware, it is perfected, awakened and freed, reaches final peace and ends all suffering. (The scan prints सव्वादुक्खाणमंतं here, where the other sutras have सव्वदुक्खाणमंतं.)
