Uttaradhyayan 16 · Brahmacharya Samadhisthan: The Supports of Celibacy

In one line: celibacy is the heart of a monk’s practice, and ten simple guards (where he lives, what he says, sees, hears, remembers, eats and wears, and how he treats the senses) keep it safe; the one who keeps it is honoured even by the gods and reaches liberation.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra Part 1, chapter 16 (12 prose sutras, then 17 verses), translated pages 368–390. 12 minutes.

What the chapter teaches

  1. Celibacy holds up the whole practice. The introduction calls celibacy (brahmacharya) the backbone of a monk’s life: austerity, study, meditation and the rest “revolve around” it like planets round the sun. Rules alone do not finish the work, since the seeds of desire lie within; but they are watchful guards that keep the seeker from drifting (pp.368, 370).
  2. The elders taught ten supports. Sudharma tells Jambu what he heard from the Lord: the elders gave ten supports for steadiness in celibacy (samadhi-sthana). Hearing them and taking them to heart, a monk grows in restraint (samyama), in stopping karma (samvara) and in calm, guards mind, speech and body, and lives always without carelessness (sutras 1–2).
  3. Each support comes with a reason. The chapter first gives the ten in prose. Each says “one who does not do this is a nirgrantha”, asks “why?”, and the teacher gives the same answer each time: doubt, longing or wavering about celibacy may arise; the vow may break; he may lose his balance of mind, fall into long illness, or fall away from the dharma taught by the omniscient ones (sutras 3–12).
  4. The ten supports in prose. (1) Live and sit in a secluded place, not one shared with women, animals or eunuchs. (2) Do not talk about women. (3) Do not sit on the same seat with women. (4) Do not gaze at and dwell on their charming features. (5) Do not listen to their sounds (singing, laughing, weeping, lamenting) from behind a wall or curtain. (6) Do not recall past pleasures and play from household life. (7) Do not eat rich food. (8) Do not eat or drink beyond measure. (9) Do not adorn the body, since an adorned monk becomes desirable to women. (10) Do not chase sound, form, taste, smell and touch (sutras 3–12).
  5. The commentary spells out the harm. It lists seven steps of decline: doubt, then desire, then doubt about the fruit of celibacy, then breaking the vow, then mental frenzy, then illness, and finally falling from the faith altogether (pp.373–374).
  6. The same ten in verse. The verses repeat the ten: a secluded, uncrowded lodging (v.1), no pleasing talk about women (v.2), no close acquaintance or frequent chat (v.3), no looking at their limbs and glances (v.4), no listening to their sounds (v.5), no recalling past laughter, play and pleasure (v.6), no rich food that quickly inflames desire (v.7), measured food, gained rightly, eaten at the right time, only to keep the disciplined life going (v.8), no adornment (v.9), and giving up the five kinds of sense pleasure (v.10).
  7. Together they are swift poison. Verses 11–13 list the ten again as things to avoid and say they are like talaputa poison to a man seeking the self. The commentary explains that this poison kills as soon as it touches the palate (v.11–13).
  8. Avoid every place of doubt. A wise monk always gives up hard-to-conquer pleasures and every situation that could put celibacy at risk, not only the ten named (v.14).
  9. Live in the garden of dharma. Steady, a charioteer of dharma who also guides others, he walks and delights in the garden of dharma, senses tamed, settled in celibacy (v.15).
  10. Gods bow to the celibate, and the path is eternal. Gods, demons, gandharvas, yakshas, rakshasas and kinnaras all bow to the one who does this hard thing. This dharma is steady, lasting and eternal, taught by the Jina; by it beings have become perfected (siddha), are becoming so, and will become so (v.16–17).

Key verses

Sutra 1 · The ten supports were taught

सुयं मे आउसं ! तेणं भगवया एवमक्खायं इह खलु थेरेहिं भगवंतेहिं
दस बंभचेर समाहिठाणा पण्णत्ता, जे भिक्खू सोच्चा, णिसम्म, संजमबहुले,
संवरबहुले, समाहिबहुले, गुत्ते, गुत्तिंदिए, गुत्तबंभयारी सया अप्पमत्ते विहरेज्जा।

O long-lived one, I have heard the Blessed One say this: here the venerable elders have taught ten supports for steadiness in celibacy. Having heard them and taken them to heart, a monk should live full of restraint, of stopping karma and of calm; guarded in mind, speech and body, with senses guarded, his celibacy guarded, always free from carelessness.

Verse 1 · A secluded lodging

जं विवित्तमणाइण्णं, रहियं इत्थी जणेण य ।
बंभचेरस्स रक्खट्ठा, आलयं तु णिसेवए ॥१॥

To protect celibacy, a monk should live in a lodging that is secluded, not crowded, and free of women (and of animals and eunuchs).

Verse 13 · Like talaputa poison

गत्तभूसणमिट्ठं च, कामभोगा य दुज्जया ।
णरस्सऽत्त गवेसिस्स, विसं तालउडं जहा ॥१३॥

(Together with the things in verses 11–12:) adorning the body, pleasing sense objects, and sensual pleasures that are hard to conquer: to a man who seeks the self, all these are like talaputa poison.

Verse 15 · The garden of dharma

धम्मारामे चरे भिक्खू, धिइमं धम्मसारही ।
धम्मारामे रए दंते, बंभचेर समाहिए ॥१५॥

A steady monk, a charioteer of dharma, should walk in the garden of dharma. Delighting in that garden, senses tamed, he stays settled in celibacy.

Verse 16 · The gods bow

देव-दाणव-गंधव्वा, जक्ख-रक्खस-किण्णरा ।
बंभयारिं णमंसंति, दुक्करं जे करंति तं ॥१६॥

Gods, demons (danavas), gandharvas, yakshas, rakshasas and kinnaras all bow to the celibate who does this difficult thing.

Verse 17 · An eternal path

एस धम्मे धुवे णिच्चे, सासए जिणदेसिए ।
सिद्धा सिज्झंति चाणेणं, सिज्झिस्संति तहावरे ॥१७॥
–त्ति बेमि ।

This dharma is steady, lasting and eternal, taught by the Jina. Through it beings have become perfected, are becoming perfected, and others too will become perfected. So I say.
