Acharang 1.9 · Upadhana-shruta (ઉપધાનશ્રુત): Mahavira’s Practice

In one line: the chapter shows that everything the earlier chapters ask of a monk, Lord Mahavira himself lived out, wandering without clothes or shelter, silent, beaten and set upon by dogs, fasting and meditating for some twelve and a half years without a single lapse into negligence.

Source: Acharang Sutra, Book 1 (First Shrutaskandha), chapter 9 (70 verses, 4 sections), translated pages 398–436 (data/translations/acharang-1/). The book numbers the verses afresh in each section, so references below read section.verse (9.3.8 = section 3, verse 8). 15 minutes.

What the chapter teaches

  1. “Upadhana” is the support of austerity, and this is the account heard of the Lord’s own practice. The introduction says spiritual upadhana is knowledge, faith, conduct and austerity, and that upadhana also means shaking off karma, as water washes a cloth. The chapter was written so that a practitioner is sure the earlier rules are not imagination but were lived by Mahavira (introduction, pp.398–399).
  2. He went forth in winter and soon gave up even his one cloth. “As I have heard it, I shall tell”: the Lord took initiation in the cold season and left at once. He resolved not to wrap himself in the divine garment placed on his shoulder; he kept it, as earlier Tirthankaras had, for thirteen months and then let it go and lived unclothed. For over four months bees and other creatures, drawn by the fragrance on his body, crawled on him and stung him (9.1.1–4, 9.1.22).
  3. He walked with eyes on the path and said nothing. He watched the ground a man’s length ahead, and children, frightened of his fixed gaze, gathered and shouted “Strike him!” He did not look sideways or behind and did not answer when spoken to. He did not engage with women who came to him, did not mix with householders, did not reply even to greetings, and so was sometimes struck with sticks by people who took offence (9.1.5–10, 9.1.21).
  4. Even before initiation he lived as a renouncer. For a little more than two years he stayed at home without drinking cold, unboiled water, living alone in thought, giving up care of the body, calm. The commentary says he stayed those two years at his elder brother Nandivardhana’s request after their parents’ death (9.1.11).
  5. He knew all six kinds of beings to be living and harmed none. Earth, water, fire, air, plants and moving beings all have life; stationary beings can be born as moving ones and moving ones as stationary, each by its own karma. Seeing this, he gave up all violence, done, caused or approved, and saw that possessions bring suffering (9.1.12–17).
  6. He ate only pure food and wanted nothing for the body. He did not eat food made for him, did not use a householder’s cloth or bowl, knew the measure of food, was not greedy for taste, did not even wipe his eye or scratch his body (9.1.18–20). “This is the way the wise, desireless Lord followed” closes each section (9.1.23).
  7. He stayed wherever he could, and slept little. Empty houses, assembly halls, water-sheds, shops, workshops, rest-houses, garden houses, cremation grounds and the foot of trees served him for nearly thirteen years, striving day and night. He did not sleep much; he woke himself, sometimes walking outside at night to throw off drowsiness (9.2.1–6).
  8. Snakes, birds, thieves, guards and angry people afflicted him; he stayed silent and calm. Asked “Who is in here?”, he would answer “I am a monk”, or keep silent, taking this as the highest dharma, and bore their anger without any thought of revenge. In bitter winter, when other ascetics wrapped themselves up or lit fires, he stood in an open shelter, sometimes going out at night to stand in meditation (9.2.7–16).
  9. In the Ladha country he was beaten and set upon by dogs. In that hard, remote land the people struck him with sticks, fists, spears, clods and potsherds, tore his flesh, threw dust on him, lifted him and dashed him down; few held back the dogs and many urged them on. Other ascetics there carried sticks against dogs; he laid aside all violence and gave up care of the body, and went through it all like an elephant at the front of battle (9.3.1–14).
  10. His austerity, alms-round and meditation. Healthy, he still ate less than his fill and took no medicine; he gave up purges, oiling, bathing, massage and cleaning his teeth. For eight months he lived on rice, ground meal and pulses; he fasted for half a month, a month, over two months, up to six months without water (9.4.1–7). He sought alms so as not to disturb hungry crows or other beggars, took whatever came, and meditated in steady postures, free of passion; in all that time “he did not commit negligence even once” (9.4.8–17).

Key passages

9.1.12–13 · All six kinds of beings are alive

पुढविं च आउकायं च, तेउकायं च वायुकायं च ।
पणगाइं बीयहरियाइं, तसकायं च सव्वसो णच्चा ॥
एयाइं संति पडिलेहे, चितमंताइ से अभिण्णाय ।
परिवज्जियाण विहरित्था, इति संखाय से महावीरे ॥

Knowing fully the earth-bodied, water-bodied, fire-bodied and air-bodied beings, moulds and fungus, seeds and green plants, and moving beings; seeing that all these exist and knowing them to be living, Mahavira understood this and wandered having given up harming them.

9.1.21 · Eyes on the path

अप्पं तिरियं पेहाए, अप्पं पिट्ठओ उ पेहाए ।
अप्पं बुइए अपडिभाणी, पंथपेही चरे जयमाणे ॥

He did not look to the side, nor look behind. Silent, not answering even when spoken to, he walked with care, watching the path.

9.2.4 · Nearly thirteen years, day and night

एतेहिं मुणी सयणेहिं, समणे आसि पतेरस वासे ।
राइंदिवं पि जयमाणे, अप्पमत्ते समाहिए झाइ ॥

In these resting places the sage, the Shramana, lived for nearly thirteen years. Striving day and night, free of negligence and composed, he meditated.

9.3.7–8 · Like an elephant at the front of battle

णिहाय दंडं पाणेहिं, तं वोसिज्ज कायमणगारे ।
अह गामकंटए भगवं, ते अहियासए अभिसमेच्चा ॥
णागो संगामसीसे वा, पारए तत्थ से महावीरे ।
एवं पि तत्थ लाढेहिं, अलद्धपुव्वो वि एगया गामो ॥

Laying aside all violence toward living beings and giving up care for his own body, the homeless Lord bore the villagers’ thorn-sharp words and blows, knowing them to shed karma. Like an elephant at the front of a battle, Mahavira went through to the far side. Even so, in Ladha, at times he did not reach even a village.

9.4.15 · Not negligent even once

अकसायी विगयगेही य सद्द-रूवेसु अमुच्छिए झाइ ।
छउमत्थे विप्परक्कममाणे, ण पमायं सइं पि कुव्वित्था ॥

Free of passions, free of craving, not infatuated by sounds, forms and the like, he meditated. Striving hard while still not omniscient (chhadmastha), he did not fall into negligence (pramada) even once.
