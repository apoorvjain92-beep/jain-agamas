Uttaradhyayan 9 · Namipravrajya (नमिप्रव्रज्या): Nami’s Renunciation

In one line: when King Nami leaves his kingdom, Indra comes disguised as a Brahmin and tests him with ten worldly duties, and Nami answers each one by turning it inward: the real city, fort, war and wealth are inside the soul.

Source: Uttaradhyayan Sutra Part 1, chapter 9 (62 verses), translated pages 222–250 (data/translations/uttaradhyayan-1/). 15 minutes.

What the chapter teaches

  1. Nami woke up through a small sound. The introduction tells his story. King Nami of Mithila (Videha) had a burning fever for six months. When the queens ground sandalwood for him, their bangles clinked and he could not bear the noise, so they kept one bangle each and the sound stopped. Nami saw it: “Where there are many, there is conflict and sorrow; where there is one, there is peace.” He is one of four kings in the book who were awakened by an outside event (individually awakened, pratyekabuddha) (introduction, pp. 222–223).
  2. He remembered his past life and left everything. Come down from the heavens and born a man, with his deluding karma calmed, Nami remembered his previous birth, put his son on the throne, and left Mithila, its army, palace and people for a solitary place. The city filled with an uproar (v.1–5).
  3. Indra tests him: “Why is the city crying?” Nami answers with a picture: a great tree in a garden gave shade and fruit to many birds; a storm has uprooted it, and the birds cry because they have lost their shelter. His going forth harms no one; self-restraint protects all six kinds of living beings (v.6–10, commentary).
  4. “Your palace is burning.” Nami: “I live happily, for I own nothing. When Mithila burns, nothing of mine burns.” For a monk who has left wife and children, nothing is dear and nothing is hateful, and great well-being comes from seeing that he is alone (v.11–16).
  5. “First build a fort and palaces.” Nami builds a different fort: faith is the city, austerity and restraint its bolt, forgiveness its wall, the three restraints (gupti) its guard; valour is the bow and austerity the arrow that pierces the armour of karma. And only a man who doubts he will arrive builds his house on the road; build your lasting home where you are going (v.17–26).
  6. “First punish the thieves and conquer the kings.” In this world the innocent are often punished and the guilty go free, Nami says, so catch the thieves inside. And the victory over one’s own self is greater than defeating a million warriors; conquer the self and the senses, anger, pride, deceit and greed are all conquered (v.27–36).
  7. “First give gifts, hold sacrifices, stay a householder.” Self-restraint, even in one who gives nothing, is better than giving a million cows every month. A month-long fast done without right understanding is not worth a sixteenth of the well-taught dharma (v.37–44).
  8. “First fill your treasury.” Mountains of gold and silver as many as Kailash would not satisfy a greedy man; desire is endless like the sky. Knowing this, practise austerity (v.45–49).
  9. “You are giving up the pleasures you have for ones you don’t.” Nami: pleasures are a thorn, poison, a cobra; even wanting them without enjoying them leads to a bad birth. Anger takes you down, pride lowers you, deceit blocks a good state, greed brings fear in both worlds (v.50–54).
  10. Indra bows. Indra drops his disguise and praises Nami: he has conquered anger, pride, deceit and greed, and so has forgiveness, gentleness, straightforwardness and freedom from greed. He will reach liberation. The wise turn away from pleasures as Nami did (v.55–62).

Key verses

14 · Nothing of mine burns

सुहं वसामो जीवामो, जेसिं मो णत्थि किंचणं ।
मिहिलाए डज्झमाणीए, ण मे डज्झइ किंचणं ॥

We live and dwell happily, we who own nothing. When Mithila burns, nothing of mine burns.

20 · The city of faith

सद्धं णगरं किच्चा, तव संवरमग्गलं ।
खंति णिउणपागारं, तिगुत्तं दुप्पधंसयं ॥

Make faith your city, austerity and restraint its bolt, and forgiveness its strong wall, guarded by the three restraints, so that the enemy, karma, cannot break it.

34 · The greatest victory

जो सहस्सं सहस्साणं, संगामे दुज्जए जिणे ।
एगं जिणेज्ज अप्पाणं, एस से परमो जओ ॥

One man may defeat a million warriors in a hard battle; another conquers only his own self. That is the supreme victory.

36 · Conquer the self, conquer all

पंचिंदियाणि कोहं, माणं मायं तहेव लोहं च ।
दुज्जयं चेव अप्पाणं, सव्वं अप्पे जिए जियं ॥

The five senses, anger, pride, deceit and greed, and the self (the mind) are hard to conquer. When the self is conquered, all of them are conquered.

48 · Desire is endless like the sky

सुवण्ण-रुव्वस्स उ पव्वया भवे, सिया हु केलाससमा असंखया ।
णरस्स लुद्धस्स ण तेहिं किंचि, इच्छा उ आगाससमा अणंतिया ॥

Even if there were countless mountains of gold and silver as big as Kailash, they would be nothing to a greedy man, for desire is endless like the sky.

53 · Pleasures are a thorn, a poison, a cobra

सल्लं कामा विसं कामा, कामा आसीविसोवमा ।
कामे य पत्थेमाणा वि, अकामा जंति दुग्गइं ॥

Sense pleasures are a thorn, sense pleasures are poison, sense pleasures are like a venomous snake. Even those who only long for them, without enjoying them, go to a bad state of birth.
