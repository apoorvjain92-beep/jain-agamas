Acharang 2.15 · Bhavana (ભાવના): Bhavana, the Five Vows

In one line: the chapter first tells Lord Mahavira’s life from his descent into the womb to omniscience, to strengthen faith, and then gives the five great vows he taught the monks, each with five supporting practices (bhavana), to strengthen conduct.

Source: Acharang Sutra, Book 2 (Second Shrutaskandha), chapter 15 (61 numbered sutras, some of them in verse), translated pages 362–412 (data/translations/acharang-2/). 18 minutes.

What the chapter teaches

  1. Why “bhavana”. The introduction calls bhavana, steady noble reflection, a boat for the monk’s life: a mind not filled with it falls away when hardships come. The first half of the chapter (Mahavira’s life) feeds the reflection on faith; the second half (the vows) feeds the reflection on conduct (pp. 362–363).
  2. Five events under one star. Mahavira’s descent from heaven, transfer from womb to womb, birth, going forth and omniscience all fell under the Uttara-phalguni constellation; his final liberation fell under Svati (s.1).
  3. Descent and the transfer of the embryo. When 75 years and 8½ months of the fourth era were left, on the sixth of the bright half of Ashadha, he came down from the Pushpottara heaven, after a life of twenty sagaropama, into the womb of the Brahmin woman Devananda, wife of Rishabhadatta, in Brahmanakundapura. He had three kinds of knowledge: he knew he would descend and that he had descended, but not the moment itself, which is too subtle (s.2). After 82 days a god, holding it to be the gods’ custom, moved the embryo into the womb of Trishala, wife of Siddhartha, in Kshatriyakundapura; this he knew before, during and after (s.3). The commentary counts the transfer among the ten wonders of this era (p. 368).
  4. Birth, name and family. He was born healthy on the thirteenth of the bright half of Chaitra; gods lit up the night, rained down jewels and flowers and performed the Tirthankara’s consecration (s.4–7). Because the family’s wealth grew from the day he was conceived, his parents named him Vardhamana (s.8). He grew up among five nurses and lived as a young man enjoying the senses without eagerness (s.9–10). He had three names: Vardhamana from his parents, Shramana (ascetic), and Mahavira, given by the gods because he bore fearful hardships (s.11). The sutras name his father, mother, uncle, brother, sister, wife Yashoda, daughter and granddaughter (s.12).
  5. Parents’ end. His parents were lay followers in the line of Parshva. At the end they confessed, repented, lay on a bed of grass, gave up food, and died by the final fast (sallekhana); they were reborn in the Achyuta heaven and will be liberated from Mahavideha (s.13).
  6. Thirty years, a year of giving, and going forth. After thirty years at home, with his parents gone and his vow fulfilled, he gave away everything (s.14). Before going forth a Tirthankara gives a crore and eight lakh gold coins every morning for a year, 388 crore 80 lakh in all, and the Lokantika gods ask him to found the order (s.15–20). Gods led by Shakra bathed and adorned him and carried him in the palanquin Chandraprabha to the Jnatakhanda garden. There, on the tenth of the dark half of Margashirsha, fasting two days without water, he took off his ornaments, pulled out his hair in five handfuls, bowed to the Siddhas and took the vow of equanimity (samayika), and mind-reading knowledge arose in him (s.21–37).
  7. Twelve years of enduring. Sending his relatives away, he resolved that for twelve years he would give up care of the body and bear every hardship from gods, men or animals with patience, forgiveness and calm (s.38). He reached Kummaragama with an hour of daylight left and lived by the highest restraint, austerity, celibacy and patience, bearing all hardships undisturbed (s.39–40).
  8. Omniscience. In the thirteenth year, on the tenth of the bright half of Vaishakha, outside Jrumbhikagrama on the north bank of the Rjuvalika river, in the field of the householder Shyamaka, near a sala tree, squatting in the cow-milking posture in the sun on a two-day waterless fast, deep in meditation, he gained complete, unobstructed, infinite knowledge and vision (kevala), and knew all the states of all beings (s.41–43).
  9. First teaching. He taught first to the gods, then to men; to Gautama and the other monks he taught the five great vows with their bhavanas and the six kinds of living beings (s.44–45). The commentary notes that the first assembly, of gods only, took no vows (pp. 393–394).
  10. The five great vows and twenty-five bhavanas. (1) No harm to any being, with care in walking, a pure mind, pure speech, care in picking up and putting down things, and eating only food looked at in the light (s.46–48). (2) No falsehood, by speaking only after thought and giving up anger, greed, fear and joking (s.49–51). (3) Not taking what is not given, by asking for limited space with thought, eating only with the guru’s permission, keeping to the space granted, asking again and again, and asking fellow monks with thought (s.52–54). (4) Celibacy, by not talking about women, not gazing at them, not recalling past pleasures, not eating too much or rich food, and not using places shared with women, animals or eunuchs (s.55–57). (5) Non-possession, by not clinging to or recoiling from any sound, sight, smell, taste or touch (s.58–60). The monk who keeps these five vows and twenty-five bhavanas as scripture, rule and path direct follows the Lord’s command (s.61).

Key passages

1 · Five events under Uttara-phalguni

तेणं कालेणं तेणं समएणं समणे भगवं महावीरे पंचहत्थुत्तरे यावि होत्था– हत्थुत्तराहिं चुए चइत्ता गब्भं वक्कंते । हत्थुत्तराहिं गब्भाओ गब्भं साहरिए । हत्थुत्तराहिं जाए । हत्थुत्तराहिं सव्वाओ सव्वत्ताए मुंडे भवित्ता अगाराओ अणगारियं पव्वइए । हत्थुत्तराहिं कसिणे पडिपुण्णे अव्वाघाए णिरावरणे अणंते अणुत्तरे केवलवरणाणदंसणे समुप्पण्णे । साइणा भगवं परिणिव्वुए ।

At that time the ascetic Lord Mahavira had five events under Uttara-phalguni: under it he came down from heaven into the womb; under it he was moved from womb to womb; under it he was born; under it he shaved his head, left home completely and became a homeless monk; under it complete, full, unobstructed, unveiled, infinite, supreme knowledge and vision arose in him. Under Svati the Lord attained final liberation.

11 · His three names

समणे भगवं महावीरे कासवगोत्ते, तस्स णं इमे तिण्णि णामधेज्जा एवमाहिज्जंति, तं जहा– अम्मापिउसंतिए वद्धमाणे, सहसम्मुइए समणे, भीमं भयभेरवं उरालं अचेलयं परीसहं सहइ त्ति कट्टु देवेहिं से णामं कयं समणे भगवं महावीरे ।

The ascetic Lord Mahavira was of the Kashyapa clan, and three names of his are told: Vardhamana, given by his parents; Shramana, which arose of itself; and, because he bore terrible, frightful, severe hardships, unclothed, the gods named him “the ascetic Lord Mahavira”.

34 · Going forth

तओ णं समणे भगवं महावीरे दाहिणेणं दाहिणं वामेणं वामं पंचमुट्ठियं लोयं करेत्ता सिद्धाणं णमोक्कारं करेइ, करेत्ता सव्वं मे अकरणिज्जं पावकम्मं ति कट्टु सामाइयं चरित्तं पडिवज्जइ, सामाइयं चरित्तं पडिवज्जेत्ता देवपरिसं च मणुयपरिसं च आलिक्खचित्तभूयमिव ठवेइ ।

Then the ascetic Lord Mahavira, having pulled out his hair in five handfuls, right hand on the right and left hand on the left, bowed to the Siddhas. Saying “All sinful action is not to be done by me”, he took up the conduct of equanimity (samayika); and the assembly of gods and the assembly of men stood still, like a painted picture.

38 · The twelve-year resolve

तओ णं समणे भगवं महावीरे पव्वइए समाणे मित्त-णाइ-सयण-संबंधिवग्गं पडिविसज्जेइ । पडिविसज्जित्ता इमं एयारूवं अभिग्गहं अभिगिण्हइ– बारस वासाइं वोसट्ठकाए चत्तदेहे जे केइ उवसग्गा समुप्पज्जंति, तं जहा– दिव्वा वा माणुसा वा तेरिच्छिया वा, ते सव्वे उवसग्गे समुप्पण्णे समाणे सम्मं सहिस्सामि, खमिस्सामि, तितिक्खिस्सामि अहियासिस्सामि ।

Having gone forth, the Lord sent away his friends, kin and relatives, and took this resolve: “For twelve years, giving up care of the body and attachment to it, whatever hardships come, from gods, men or animals, I will bear them rightly, forgive them, endure them calmly, and endure them gladly.”

46 · The first great vow

पढमं भंते ! महव्वयं पच्चक्खामि सव्वं पाणाइवायं । से सुहुमं वा बायरं वा तसं वा थावरं वा णेव सयं पाणाइवायं करेज्जा णेवण्णेहिं पाणाइवायं कारवेज्जा, णेवण्णं पाणाइवायं करंतं समणुजाणेज्जा जावज्जीवाए तिविहं तिविहेणं मणसा वयसा कायसा तस्स भंते ! पडिक्कमामि णिंदामि गरिहामि अप्पाणं वोसिरामि ।

Venerable one, in the first great vow I give up all harm to life. Whether to subtle or gross, moving or still beings, I will not harm life myself, will not have others harm it, and will not approve of another harming it, for as long as I live, in the three ways, by mind, speech and body. Of that, venerable one, I repent, I condemn it, I censure it before the guru, and I let go of that self.

59 · Senses meet their objects; drop the liking and disliking

तत्थिमा पढमा भावणा– सोयओ णं जीवे मणुण्णामणुण्णाइं सद्दाइं सुणेइ, मणुण्णामणुण्णेहिं सद्देहिं णो सज्जेज्जा णो रज्जेज्जा णो गिज्झेज्जा णो मुज्झेज्जा णो अज्झोववज्जेज्जा णो विणिग्घायमावज्जेज्जा ।
ण सक्का ण सोउं सद्दा, सोयविसयमागया ।
राग दोसा उ जे तत्थ, ते भिक्खू परिवज्जए ॥

The first bhavana of the fifth vow: through the ear a living being hears pleasant and unpleasant sounds; he should not cling to them, delight in them, crave them, be infatuated or lost in them, or be shaken by them. Sounds that reach the ear cannot go unheard; but the liking and disliking that arise there, the monk gives up. (The same is then said of sight, smell, taste and touch.)
